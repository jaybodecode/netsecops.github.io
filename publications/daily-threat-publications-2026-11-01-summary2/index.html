Amateur Hacker Uses AI Chatbots to Breach Nine South Korean Banks

A 26-year-old amateur hacker from southern China has reportedly used publicly available AI chatbots to orchestrate a series of cyberattacks against nine major South Korean banks. The breaches resulted in the exposure of financial data for tens of thousands of customers. According to cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike, which investigated the incident, the attacker's lack of operational security allowed for their identification. The individual allegedly used AI not only to execute the attacks but also to attempt to sell the stolen data and create a fraudulent résumé. This event is part of an alarming trend of AI-facilitated cybercrime, enabling less-skilled actors to launch sophisticated attacks and raising concerns among national security officials globally.

'Black October' Attacks Steal API Keys from Nine AI Dev Platforms

A coordinated wave of attacks dubbed "Black October" has compromised at least nine AI developer platforms, including Northflank, Railway, and Lightning AI. Security researchers at Wiz, who identified the campaign, report that attackers targeted environment variables to steal API keys and other secrets. This technique, termed "LLMJacking," allows threat actors to hijack expensive AI model services for their own use, leading to significant fraudulent charges for victims. Northflank confirmed the breach after being alerted by Wiz, noting that multiple customers had reported unauthorized use of their AI API keys. The campaign highlights a critical credential crisis in AI infrastructure, with some attacks employing "agent swarm" tactics and resulting in at least one platform, Zeabur, shutting down permanently.

Russian Statistics Agency Rosstat Hit by Coordinated Cyberattack

Russia's state statistics agency, Rosstat, was targeted by a coordinated cyberattack between October 29 and November 1, 2026. The hacktivist groups Belarusian Cyber Partisans and Silent Crow claimed responsibility, defacing Rosstat's websites and alleging the theft of 23 terabytes of sensitive data. The stolen data purportedly includes information on Russian wartime losses, demographic statistics, and internal documents. Rosstat has denied any data was compromised, stating its monitoring systems detected the attack in time to prevent a breach. The agency's main website remained inaccessible following the incident. Both groups involved are banned in Russia as extremist organizations.

NetamLoader Malware Targets Asian Governments in Supply Chain Attack

A previously undocumented malware framework named NetamLoader has been identified in a sophisticated campaign targeting government organizations in Asia. According to research from Kaspersky, the malware is delivered through a Windows cabinet (CAB) file placed in an OS update directory, suggesting a potential supply chain attack. The primary infection mechanism is AppDomainManager injection, a stealthy technique that hijacks the .NET Framework's startup process to load the malicious payload within a legitimate application's context. This method allows the malware to evade detection, similar to DLL sideloading. The campaign specifically targets a custom build of the Windows 10 operating system used by the targeted government entities. Attribution and the full extent of the compromise remain unknown.

Ransomware Gangs Qilin and Barracuda Claim New Corporate Victims

The ransomware landscape remains highly active, with the Qilin and Barracuda groups claiming new victims on November 1, 2026. The Qilin gang, one of 2026's most prolific groups, added U.S.-based company Danser to its list of victims. Meanwhile, the Barracuda ransomware group announced it had breached EVINOKS, a Turkish technology and manufacturing firm. Barracuda threatened to release the stolen data from EVINOKS for free on its dark web leak site. These incidents are part of a broader surge in ransomware attacks, which saw a 45% increase in the first half of 2026, with the manufacturing sector being the most frequent target.

Critical Memory Corruption Flaws Disclosed in Microsoft Edge

An advisory released on October 31, 2026, details multiple critical and high-severity vulnerabilities in Microsoft Edge (Chromium-based) versions prior to 156.0.4314.58. These flaws, inherited from the open-source Chromium project, include several memory corruption bugs. The most severe, CVE-2026-118306, is a critical memory corruption issue in the FileSystem component that could allow a remote attacker to execute arbitrary code. Another high-severity flaw, CVE-2026-118305, is a similar memory corruption bug in the Turbolev component. Other vulnerabilities could lead to code execution within the sandbox or bypass of the web origin policy. Users are urged to update their browsers immediately to the patched version to mitigate the risk of exploitation.

China-Linked APT Exploits PHP2Shell and Fragnesia to Root Firewalls

Update:New analysis confirms the China-nexus group is specifically targeting Palo Alto Networks (PAN-OS) firewalls. After gaining root access via PHP2Shell and Fragnesia, the attackers are pivoting from the compromised firewalls to conduct reconnaissance and credential theft against internal Microsoft Active Directory domains, indicating a broader network intrusion objective. The group also utilizes an AI-assisted framework named ARTEX. CISA has added Fragnesia (CVE-2026-46300) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, mandating a patch deadline for federal agencies.
