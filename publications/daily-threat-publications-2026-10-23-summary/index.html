Today New Articles

Capcom Data Breach Exposes Data of Over 11 Million Users

Japanese gaming company Capcom has confirmed a significant data breach of its CAPCOM ID membership service, impacting over 11.7 million customer accounts. The incident, detected on October 19, 2026, resulted in the unauthorized access and theft of user data including email addresses, IP addresses, and login information. While financial data was not compromised, Capcom is warning all users to be vigilant against phishing attempts. The company has notified authorities and is in the process of contacting affected individuals.

Arista Patches Exploited VeloCloud Orchestrator Zero-Day

Arista Networks has released emergency patches for a critical zero-day vulnerability, CVE-2026-114625, in its VeloCloud Orchestrator (VCO) on-premises solution. The flaw, a SQL injection that allows for privilege escalation, is being actively exploited in the wild. Successful exploitation allows an authenticated user to gain superuser control over the SD-WAN infrastructure. Arista has identified malicious IPs and urges customers to patch immediately.

Ransomware Attack Forces NY School District to Cancel Classes

The Oneonta City School District in New York canceled classes for two days following a ransomware attack discovered on its network. The attack has affected five schools and around 1,700 students. The district's technology team is working with local, state, and federal authorities to investigate the incident, contain the damage, and restore systems. While classes are canceled, athletic events are proceeding as scheduled.

INTERPOL Disrupts 10,909 Malicious Networks, Arrests 1,462

A global law enforcement action led by INTERPOL, codenamed Operation Serengeti III, has resulted in the disruption of 10,909 malicious networks and the arrest of 1,462 suspects across 21 countries. The operation, which ran from June to August 2026, targeted a wide range of cybercrime activities and successfully recovered over $105 million in illicit funds. The total financial losses linked to the dismantled networks are estimated to exceed $385 million.

New 'GlassWorm' Malware Spreads Via VS Code Extensions

A new self-propagating malware called GlassWorm is targeting developers by hiding in malicious Visual Studio (VS) Code extensions. The malware acts as both a worm and a credential stealer, designed to exfiltrate saved browser logins, developer tokens (e.g., for GitHub, AWS), and cryptocurrency wallet data. Its worm-like capabilities allow it to spread to other systems, posing a significant supply chain risk if it compromises developer machines with access to sensitive code repositories.

Critical Splunk Enterprise Flaw Allows System Compromise

A critical improper access control vulnerability, CVE-2026-76299, has been discovered in Splunk Enterprise, carrying a CVSS score of 9.9. The flaw allows a low-privileged network attacker to compromise affected systems with no user interaction required. A successful exploit could have a high impact on the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the Splunk instance. Cisco, which discovered the flaw internally, has released security hardening updates and strongly urges users to patch immediately.

Advisory Warns of Prolific SafePay Ransomware Targeting Critical Infrastructure

Update:Further analysis of the SafePay ransomware group reveals they employ double extortion tactics, exfiltrating data before encryption using tools such as 7-Zip, WinRAR, FileZilla, and RClone. Initial access methods now specifically include exploitation of Citrix and Fortinet products, and attackers use Mullvad VPN for anonymity. Lateral movement is facilitated by tools like PsExec, AnyDesk, and TeamViewer. These new details provide a more granular understanding of the threat actor's capabilities and specific attack vectors, enhancing mitigation strategies.

Kroll Report: 69% of Orgs Breached in Past Year, Most via Third Parties

Update:The updated analysis of Kroll's 2026 Data Breach Outlook report provides a crucial new statistic: only 7% of breaches originated directly in the technology sector, yet these incidents were responsible for a staggering 79% of all individuals impacted by third-party compromises. This highlights the disproportionate 'domino effect' of tech vendor breaches. The article also expands on regulatory implications, offering more structured compliance requirements and tactical guidance for robust vendor risk management, including the importance of understanding fourth-party risks and strengthening contractual obligations with vendors. It reinforces the perception gap between IT leaders and C-suite executives regarding third-party risk.
