## Articles in this publication
- [Ransomware Gangs Qilin and Barracuda Claim New Corporate Victims](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/ransomware-gangs-qilin-and-barracuda-claim-new-victims/) (high)
  The ransomware landscape remains highly active, with the Qilin and Barracuda groups claiming new victims on November 1, 2026. The Qilin gang, one of 2026's most prolific groups, added U.S.-based company Danser to its list of victims. Meanwhile, the Barracuda ransomware group announced it had breached EVINOKS, a Turkish technology and manufacturing firm. Barracuda threatened to release the stolen data from EVINOKS for free on its dark web leak site. These incidents are part of a broader surge in ransomware attacks, which saw a 45% increase in the first half of 2026, with the manufacturing sector being the most frequent target.
- [Forescout Research: AI Finds 66 New TCP/IP Stack Flaws in 90 Hours](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/forescout-research-ai-finds-critical-flaws-20x-faster/) (medium)
  Groundbreaking research from Forescout's Vedere Labs demonstrates that commercial AI models can find critical vulnerabilities in foundational networking software up to 20 times faster than traditional manual methods. The AI-assisted process identified 99 vulnerabilities, including 66 previously unknown flaws, in widely used TCP/IP stacks in just 90 hours—a task that would typically take nine months. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for finding impactful bugs for both defenders and attackers.
- [Chinese APT Exploits Critical VMware vCenter Zero-Day for Months](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/chinese-apt-armory-castle-exploits-critical-vmware-vcenter-zero-day/) (critical)
  A China-linked advanced persistent threat (APT) group, identified as Armory Castle, has been exploiting a critical zero-day vulnerability in VMware vCenter for over two months before a patch was available. The vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-123890 with a CVSS score of 9.8, allows unauthorized access and the forging of authentication tokens. The attackers used the flaw to create rogue administrator accounts and exfiltrate sensitive infrastructure data, highlighting significant risks in enterprise virtualization environments. Broadcom (VMware's parent company) released a patch on November 19, 2026, and organizations are urged to apply it immediately.
- [New White House Executive Order Modifies Cybersecurity Mandates](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/white-house-issues-new-cybersecurity-executive-order-modifying-prior-policies/) (informational)
  The White House has issued a new executive order, 'Sustaining Select Efforts to Strengthen the Nation's Cybersecurity,' which amends certain policies from previous directives. The new order removes a specific mandate for federal agencies to implement phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (MFA), although the requirement remains part of the broader Federal Zero Trust Strategy. It also alters software supply chain validation requirements for government contractors, shifting from upfront artifact submission to a post-incident enforcement model. The order signals a continued, but adjusted, focus on national cybersecurity priorities.
- [JPMorgan, Visa, Mastercard Exposed in Third-Party Vendor Breach](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/jpmorgan-visa-mastercard-report-data-exposure-from-vendor-breach/) (high)
  JPMorgan, Visa, and Mastercard have all confirmed customer data was exposed due to a security breach at a shared, unnamed third-party vendor. The incidents highlight the significant and persistent risks within the financial services supply chain. The companies have stated the breaches are unrelated to other recent technology outages. While the full scope of the data exposure has not been disclosed, such third-party breaches are a major attack vector, as criminals often target smaller, less secure service providers to gain access to the data of large corporations.
- [Identity Issues and Misconfigurations Cause Most 2026 Cloud Breaches](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/cloud-breaches-in-2026-caused-by-identity-issues-and-misconfigurations/) (informational)
  Recent security reports indicate that the overwhelming majority of cloud security incidents in 2026 are caused by identity-based attacks and simple misconfigurations, rather than sophisticated exploits. One report found that identity issues—such as stolen credentials, phishing, and over-permissioned accounts—were the root cause of 83% of major cloud breaches. Misconfigurations, like unsecured storage buckets and insecure APIs, remain a leading threat, cited by 68% of organizations. This highlights that while cloud platforms are generally secure, the responsibility of proper configuration and identity management falls on the customer, creating significant room for human error.
