Microsoft Patches Exploited CVSS 10.0 Zero-Day in Entra ID Service

CRITICAL

Microsoft Entra ID Zero-Day (CVE-2026-69836) Patched

Microsoft has deployed a server-side patch for a critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerability in its Entra ID cloud identity service, tracked as CVE-2026-69836. The flaw, which has a maximum CVSS score of 10.0, was confirmed to have been exploited as a zero-day before Microsoft's internal discovery and mitigation. The vulnerability stemmed from the deserialization of untrusted data, allowing unauthenticated attackers to execute code remotely. As the fix was applied on Microsoft's backend, no customer action is required. Details regarding the attackers or the scope of exploitation have not been disclosed. A compromise of Entra ID could grant attackers widespread access to Microsoft 365, Azure, and other connected cloud environments.

GitLab Flaw (CVE-2026-19478) Actively Exploited to Attack Projects

CRITICAL

GitLab Vulnerability (CVE-2026-19478) Exploited in Wild

A critical code injection vulnerability in GitLab, CVE-2026-19478, is being actively exploited just days after its disclosure. The flaw, rated 9.4, allows unauthenticated remote attackers to modify or delete public projects on self-managed GitLab instances. The vulnerability lies in the handling of a GraphQL directive. Security firm watchTowr observed exploitation attempts on its honeypots shortly after the patch was released, indicating rapid weaponization. A successful exploit could lead to repository deletion, forged merge records, and banned maintainers, posing a severe software supply chain risk. GitLab has released patches for affected CE and EE versions, and administrators are urged to update immediately or restrict access to the `/api/graphql` endpoint.

NetScaler ADC and Gateway Hit by Critical Auth Bypass (CVE-2026-19490)

CRITICAL

NetScaler Auth Bypass Flaw (CVE-2026-19490) Patched

Cloud Software Group has patched a critical authentication bypass vulnerability, CVE-2026-19490, in NetScaler ADC and Gateway. The flaw, rated 9.3, allows a remote, unauthenticated attacker to bypass authentication on appliances configured as a Gateway (SSL VPN, etc.) or AAA virtual server. This could grant attackers access to protected internal services without credentials. Given NetScaler's common deployment at the network edge, these devices are high-value targets. While not yet exploited in the wild, security experts anticipate rapid weaponization. Customers are strongly urged to apply the patches. A second high-severity DoS flaw (CVE-2026-19489) was also fixed.

Oracle's August Update Delivers Massive 943 Patches for 925 CVEs

HIGH

Oracle August 2026 CSPU Includes 943 Security Patches

Oracle has released its August 2026 Critical Security Patch Update (CSPU), a massive release containing 943 patches for 925 unique CVEs across 23 product families. The Oracle Fusion Middleware and Hyperion product lines received the most fixes, each with 262. A significant portion of these are for critical, remotely exploitable vulnerabilities that require no user authentication. Nearly 90 of the fixed flaws have a CVSS score of 9.8 or higher. Oracle WebLogic Server was notably affected, with several critical bugs like CVE-2026-60702 (CVSS 9.9) being exploitable via T3, IIOP, and RMI protocols. Given the high number of critical flaws, organizations are urged to prioritize the deployment of these patches, especially on internet-facing systems.

AI-Powered Cyberattacks Target Taiwan and Cryptocurrency Users

HIGH

AI Used in Attacks on Taiwan and Crypto Users

Threat actors are increasingly leveraging Artificial Intelligence (AI) in sophisticated cyberattacks. One campaign involved a near-autonomous attack against Taiwanese government systems, where up to eight AI agents collaborated to map networks, find vulnerabilities, and exfiltrate over 2,500 personnel records. In a separate campaign named Operation ASTERIX, attackers used the Claude Code AI model to screen over 100,000 phone numbers to identify high-value targets for a cryptocurrency phishing scheme. These incidents demonstrate AI's growing role in automating various stages of the attack lifecycle, from reconnaissance and target selection to lateral movement, posing a significant challenge to traditional defense mechanisms.

Krybit Ransomware Group Claims Attack on Singaporean Healthcare Firm

HIGH

Krybit Ransomware Hits Singapore Healthcare Company

The Krybit ransomware group has claimed responsibility for an attack on a healthcare company in Singapore. Employing a double-extortion strategy, the threat actors have encrypted the victim's systems and exfiltrated data, which they are now threatening to leak to compel a ransom payment. The attack reportedly targeted the company's web applications as the initial access point. This incident highlights the persistent threat that ransomware poses to the healthcare sector, where operational continuity and the confidentiality of patient data are paramount. A successful attack can lead to severe operational disruption, data loss, and significant reputational damage.

New 'Majinahanashi' Ransomware Group Hits Hospitality Sector

HIGH

Majinahanashi Ransomware Targets Hotels in UK and Malaysia

Update:

Further analysis of the Majinahanashi ransomware reveals advanced evasion techniques. The malware employs dynamic API resolution and direct system calls to bypass traditional EDR hooking, making detection more challenging. Additionally, strings within the malware are XOR-obfuscated to hinder static analysis, and it performs checks for keyboard layout and geography to avoid execution in certain regions, a common tactic for malware originating from CIS countries. These new details highlight the group's increasing sophistication and challenge for defenders.
