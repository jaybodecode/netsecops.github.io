# Ransomware Surges, Data Breaches Expose Millions

**Published:** 2026-07-26 | **Articles:** 2

Ransomware attacks have seen a significant increase of 25%, with 146 threat groups now active, according to an updated analysis of Black Kite's 2026 Ransomware Report. Attackers are increasingly employing AI for sophisticated social engineering, including deepfake audio in vishing campaigns. Defenders are advised to monitor for specific command-line activities and failed logon events. Mitigation strategies now emphasize Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for all remote access and privileged accounts, alongside continuous user training to counter AI-driven phishing and vishing.

In parallel, multiple high-impact data breaches have affected millions. A significant incident at 700Credit, an auto industry credit check provider, exposed the personal data of 5.6 million individuals, including Social Security numbers. Separately, a database of nearly 200,000 supporters of Hungary's Tisza Party was compromised, triggering a government investigation. The Anubis ransomware group also targeted Coca-Cola's subsidiary Fairlife, among other breaches, underscoring ongoing threats to sensitive data across diverse sectors.

  Black Kite's 2026 Ransomware Report reveals a 24.9% year-over-year increase in victims, with 7,551 attacks disclosed between April 2025 and March 2026. The ecosystem has fragmented into 146 active groups, yet the top five, led by the prolific Qilin gang, are responsible for over 43% of all incidents. The manufacturing sector remains the hardest hit, and the U.S. accounts for nearly half of all victims. The report also notes a concerning trend of victims failing to patch critical vulnerabilities post-breach.
- [Multiple Data Breaches Impact Millions, from Auto Finance to Politics](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/bright-defense-compiles-recent-high-impact-data-breaches-2026/) (high)
  A report from Bright Defense on July 26, 2026, details several high-impact data breaches. A major incident at 700Credit, an auto industry credit check provider, exposed the personal data of 5.6 million individuals, including Social Security numbers. Another significant leak affected Hungary's Tisza Party, compromising a database of nearly 200,000 supporters and prompting a government investigation. The report also notes a ransomware attack by the Anubis group on Coca-Cola's subsidiary Fairlife and other breaches, highlighting a persistent threat to sensitive data across various sectors.
