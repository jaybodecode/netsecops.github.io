# AI Attacks Expand, Critical Flaws in ESG & Android Patched

**Published:** 2026-11-04 | **Articles:** 7

**Critical Vulnerabilities Under Active Exploitation:**

*   **'Black October' Attacks Steal API Keys from Nine AI Dev Platforms:** The ongoing campaign has expanded to over a dozen AI developer platforms, with threat actors using AI-assisted tools like the Artex framework. Initial access vectors are diverse, including compromised credentials, vulnerable dependencies, and zero-day exploits in the Zammad ticketing system.
*   **European Supermarkets Suffer Second Wave of Refrigeration Cyberattacks:** The cyberattacks have now impacted Finland, Luxembourg, and the Netherlands, with the Coop chain identified as an additional victim. Authorities are considering the incident a potential act of hybrid warfare targeting critical food supply infrastructure, exploiting vulnerabilities in Building Management Systems.
*   **Barracuda Warns of Critical Flaws Allowing Root Access in ESG:** Barracuda Networks is urging immediate patching of two vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-116606 and CVE-2026-111560) in its Email Security Gateway appliances. These flaws can be chained by an unauthenticated attacker to bypass authorization and execute commands with root privileges, a significant increase in threat level.

**New Threats and Advisories:**

*   **APT29 Targets iOS & Android Users in 'Poisoned Source' Campaign:** The Russian SVR-linked group APT29 is conducting an intelligence-gathering campaign using watering hole attacks and a supply chain compromise of the Ukrainian firm Plerdy. Malicious code was injected to fingerprint devices and deliver exploit chains, including the DarkSword kit for iOS, to victims in Ukraine and the United States.
*   **Chinese APT 'SlippyNunchucks' Hijacks Sogou App for Malware:** The China-aligned group 'SlippyNunchucks' is abusing the Sogou Input Method editor through DNS hijacking. An insecure HTTP communication flaw (CVE-2026-120422) in the application's vocabulary promotion mechanism allows them to deliver their 'DarkVigil' malware to targets across Asia and Europe.

**Policy & Industry Notes:**

*   **UK NCSC: AI-Powered Ransomware on Critical Infrastructure Likely by 2028:** The NCSC's annual report warns of severe societal disruption from AI-powered ransomware targeting UK Critical National Infrastructure (CNI). The report highlights technical debt and end-of-life systems as vulnerabilities, prompting the UK government to announce funding for a national Cyber Shield and a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability (NERV) Centre.
*   **Android's November Patch Fixes Critical Zero-Click RCE, 'SMS of Death':** The November 2026 Android security update addresses 119 vulnerabilities in core OS components, including the critical CVE-2026-58868. Further patches cover vendor-specific components, and new detection methods for checking security patch levels have been provided.

## Articles in this publication
- ['Black October' Attacks Steal API Keys from Nine AI Dev Platforms](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/black-october-attacks-hit-nine-ai-developer-platforms/) (high)
  A coordinated wave of attacks dubbed "Black October" has compromised at least nine AI developer platforms, including Northflank, Railway, and Lightning AI. Security researchers at Wiz, who identified the campaign, report that attackers targeted environment variables to steal API keys and other secrets. This technique, termed "LLMJacking," allows threat actors to hijack expensive AI model services for their own use, leading to significant fraudulent charges for victims. Northflank confirmed the breach after being alerted by Wiz, noting that multiple customers had reported unauthorized use of their AI API keys. The campaign highlights a critical credential crisis in AI infrastructure, with some attacks employing "agent swarm" tactics and resulting in at least one platform, Zeabur, shutting down permanently.
- [European Supermarkets Suffer Second Wave of Refrigeration Cyberattacks](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/european-supermarkets-hit-by-second-refrigeration-cyberattack/) (high)
  A coordinated cyberattack on November 2, 2026, targeted supermarket refrigeration systems across at least seven European countries, remotely raising temperatures and causing millions of euros in food spoilage. The attack exploited vulnerabilities in internet-connected controllers from Danfoss and Copeland, echoing a similar incident from three weeks prior. This second wave suggests a deliberate campaign to disrupt the European food supply chain, with authorities investigating the incidents as a serious threat to economic stability and consumer confidence.
- [UK NCSC: AI-Powered Ransomware on Critical Infrastructure Likely by 2028](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/uk-ncsc-warns-of-ai-powered-ransomware-attack-on-critical-infrastructure/) (informational)
  The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has warned that an AI-powered ransomware attack targeting the nation's critical infrastructure is 'highly likely' by 2028. In its annual 'State of the Cyber Nation' report, the GCHQ division stated that AI will not invent new attack types but will dramatically increase the speed and scale at which existing vulnerabilities can be exploited. The NCSC anticipates an 'unprecedented rise' in cyber incidents and urges organizations to focus on fundamental cybersecurity hygiene to defend against these accelerated threats.
- [Android's November Patch Fixes Critical Zero-Click RCE, 'SMS of Death'](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/android-november-2026-security-update-patches-critical-zero-click-rce/) (critical)
  Google and Samsung have rolled out the November 2026 Android security update, addressing over 200 vulnerabilities. The patch fixes several critical flaws, including CVE-2026-58868, a zero-click remote code execution (RCE) vulnerability in the Android Framework that requires no user interaction to exploit. Another critical fix, CVE-2026-58882, resolves the 'SMS of Death' bug that could allow an attacker to remotely crash devices via a text message. The update covers Android versions 14 through 17 and includes specific patches for Samsung Galaxy devices.
- [APT29 Targets iOS & Android Users in 'Poisoned Source' Campaign](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/apt29-targets-ios-android-users-in-watering-hole-supply-chain-attack/) (critical)
  The Russian SVR-linked threat group APT29 (aka Midnight Blizzard, Cozy Bear) has been identified as the actor behind a large-scale intelligence-gathering campaign targeting iOS and Android users. According to research from Sekoia, the 'Poisoned Source' operation used multiple vectors, including watering hole attacks on high-profile websites and a supply chain compromise of the Ukrainian web analytics firm Plerdy. The attackers injected malicious code to fingerprint devices and deliver n-day exploit chains, including the DarkSword kit for iOS, to victims primarily in Ukraine and the United States.
- [Barracuda Warns of Critical Flaws Allowing Root Access in ESG](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/barracuda-warns-of-critical-vulnerabilities-in-esg-appliances/) (critical)
  Barracuda Networks is urging customers to immediately patch two vulnerabilities in its Email Security Gateway (ESG) appliances. The flaws, CVE-2026-116606 and CVE-2026-111560, can be chained together by an unauthenticated attacker to bypass authorization and execute commands with root privileges. An updated advisory clarified that administrative privileges are not required for exploitation, significantly increasing the threat level. This follows a history of ESG appliances being targeted by sophisticated threat actors for espionage.
- [Chinese APT 'SlippyNunchucks' Hijacks Sogou App for Malware](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/china-linked-slippynunchucks-apt-hijacks-sogou-app-to-distribute-malware/) (high)
  The China-aligned threat group 'SlippyNunchucks' is conducting a surveillance campaign by abusing the popular Sogou Input Method editor, developed by Tencent. According to ESET Research, the group uses DNS hijacking to exploit an insecure HTTP communication flaw (CVE-2026-120422) in the application's vocabulary promotion mechanism. This allows them to deliver their 'DarkVigil' malware to targets in Hong Kong, Thailand, Japan, and other countries in Asia and Europe. Tencent has since released a patch to migrate the affected communication to HTTPS.
