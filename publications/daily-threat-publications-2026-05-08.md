# Massive Canvas LMS Breach, Unpatched Linux Zero-Day, and Palo Alto Firewall Exploits Rattle Global Organizations

**Published:** 2026-05-08 | **Articles:** 10

This 24-hour period has been marked by severe, high-impact cybersecurity incidents, led by a catastrophic data breach of the Canvas LMS affecting millions of students and faculty worldwide during final exams. Adding to the pressure, a critical, unpatched 'Dirty Frag' zero-day vulnerability was disclosed for the Linux kernel, enabling immediate root access. Meanwhile, threat actors are actively exploiting zero-days in Palo Alto Networks firewalls and a Windows Shell flaw, while new ransomware (BARADAI) and cloud worm (PCPJack) campaigns have emerged. These events highlight escalating threats from supply chain attacks, sophisticated phishing, and the rapid weaponization of vulnerabilities.

## Articles in this publication
- [Ransomware Landscape Report: Qilin Leads, 'The Gentlemen' Surges in Q1 2026](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/ransomware-landscape-shifts-as-qilin-and-the-gentlemen-groups-surge-in-q1-2026/) (informational)
  The first quarter of 2026 shows that the high volume of ransomware attacks seen in late 2025 has become the new baseline. Research from GuidePoint Security indicates that while overall numbers have stabilized at this elevated level, the landscape of dominant players is shifting. The Qilin ransomware group was the most active, claiming 361 victims, despite a 25% decrease in its activity. A newer group, known as 'The Gentlemen,' saw a dramatic surge, becoming the second most active group with 182 victims. The manufacturing sector remains the primary target, with a notable 44% year-over-year increase in attacks on the construction industry.
- [Actively Exploited Windows Zero-Day (CVE-2026-32202) Steals NTLM Hashes Without User Clicks](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/microsoft-warns-actively-exploited-windows-zero-day-cve-2026-32202-credential-theft/) (critical)
  Microsoft and CISA are warning of active, zero-click exploitation of a Windows Shell spoofing vulnerability, CVE-2026-32202. The flaw, which stems from an incomplete patch for a bug previously used by APT28, allows attackers to steal Net-NTLMv2 hashes simply by having a user open a folder containing a malicious file. This enables NTLM relay and pass-the-hash attacks. CISA has added the vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, mandating that U.S. federal agencies apply the patch immediately.
- [Microsoft Details Phishing Campaign Targeting 35,000 Users](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/microsoft-details-large-scale-phishing-campaign-targeting-35000-users/) (high)
  Microsoft has detailed a large-scale and sophisticated phishing campaign that targeted over 35,000 users in 26 countries between April 14 and 16, 2026. The attack used polished 'code of conduct' lures and an adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) phishing kit to steal session tokens and bypass multi-factor authentication. The campaign primarily targeted organizations in the United States, with the healthcare, financial services, and professional services sectors being the most affected. The report also highlights a 146% surge in QR code phishing attacks in Q1 2026.
- [Critical Palo Alto Networks Zero-Day (CVE-2026-0300) Actively Exploited for RCE](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/palo-alto-networks-pan-os-zero-day-cve-2026-0300-under-active-exploitation/) (critical)
  Palo Alto Networks has issued an urgent advisory for a critical, unpatched zero-day vulnerability, CVE-2026-0300, in its PAN-OS software. The flaw, a buffer overflow in the User-ID Authentication Portal, allows for unauthenticated remote code execution with root privileges. The company has confirmed limited but active exploitation in the wild, targeting internet-exposed firewalls. With a CVSS score of 9.3, the vulnerability affects multiple PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls. Patches are not yet available, with the first wave expected around May 13, 2026. Palo Alto Networks and global cybersecurity agencies are urging immediate implementation of mitigation measures, which involve restricting access to the vulnerable portal.
- [CISA Considers Slashing Critical Vulnerability Patching Deadline to 72 Hours](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/cisa-considers-slashing-critical-vulnerability-patching-deadline-to-3-days/) (informational)
  The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) is reportedly considering a drastic policy change that would shorten the remediation timeline for critical vulnerabilities from 14 days to just 72 hours for federal agencies. This potential move applies to flaws listed in the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog. The primary driver for this proposal is the growing concern among federal officials that advanced AI models, like Anthropic's 'Claude Mythos', will dramatically accelerate the speed at which exploits are developed, collapsing the window between disclosure and mass exploitation. The proposal has been met with debate, as experts question the feasibility of such a rapid turnaround for testing and deployment in large government systems.
- [Critical Unpatched 'Dirty Frag' Linux Zero-Day Allows Instant Root Access](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/critical-linux-zero-day-dirty-frag-cve-2026-43284-disclosed-without-patch/) (critical)
  A critical, unpatched zero-day vulnerability in the Linux kernel, dubbed 'Dirty Frag' and tracked as CVE-2026-43284, has been publicly disclosed. The flaw, which has reportedly existed for nine years within the kernel's `algif_aead` cryptographic interface, allows a local attacker to achieve immediate root privilege escalation on all major Linux distributions. The disclosure was made without a patch at the request of the Linux Distros Openwall maintainers, placing immense pressure on system administrators to find mitigations. The vulnerability is described as more severe than the recently discovered 'Copy Fail' (CVE-2026-31431) flaw and highlights a worrying trend of long-dormant, high-impact bugs in the Linux kernel.
- [New 'BARADAI' Ransomware Emerges, Demanding Up to $80,000](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/new-baradai-ransomware-targets-multiple-industries-worldwide/) (high)
  Researchers at CYFIRMA have identified a new ransomware strain named BARADAI that is actively targeting Windows systems. The malware encrypts files on local and network drives, appending a unique extension and dropping a ransom note with contact instructions. Observed victims are located in the USA, Brazil, France, Australia, Italy, Israel, and Malaysia, with a focus on the education, manufacturing, retail, and logistics sectors. Ransom demands have ranged from $10,000 to $80,000. BARADAI establishes persistence via `CurrentVersion\Run` registry keys and manipulates system settings for defense evasion. Currently, no public decryption tool is available, and experts warn that future versions may adopt double extortion tactics.
- [Fraudulent 'CallPhantom' Apps on Google Play Scammed 7.3 Million Users](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/fraudulent-callphantom-apps-on-google-play-scam-7-3-million-users/) (medium)
  ESET researchers have exposed a massive fraud operation on the Google Play Store named 'CallPhantom.' The campaign involved 28 fraudulent apps that were collectively downloaded over 7.3 million times, primarily targeting users in India and the Asia-Pacific region. The apps promised the ability to access anyone's call history, SMS records, and WhatsApp logs for a subscription fee. However, after payment, the apps only provided fake, randomly generated data. One app was even published under the deceptive developer name 'Indian gov.in' to appear legitimate. Google has since removed the fleeceware applications from the Play Store.
- [New Cloud Worm 'PCPJack' Steals Secrets, Eradicates Rival Malware](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/new-cloud-worm-pcpjack-steals-secrets-and-replaces-rival-malware/) (high)
  Researchers at SentinelLabs have discovered a new modular cloud worm named 'PCPJack' that actively steals credentials and secrets from a wide range of cloud services. The malware targets secrets for AWS, GitHub, Slack, Kubernetes, and various cryptocurrency exchanges. In a unique twist, PCPJack is programmed to first detect and remove any existing infections from a rival threat actor, 'TeamPCP,' before installing itself. For propagation, the worm uses stolen secrets for lateral movement via SSH, Kubernetes, and Docker, and also downloads data from Common Crawl to identify new, exposed cloud services to attack. This behavior indicates a competitive and sophisticated threat targeting cloud environments.
- [CISO Role Crisis: Demands, Legal Risks, and Shortages Make Position Untenable](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/ciso-role-becoming-untenable-amidst-growing-demands-and-legal-risks/) (informational)
  The role of the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is becoming increasingly untenable in 2026, according to a new report from the United States Cybersecurity Institute. A severe shortage of qualified professionals is being exacerbated by expanding job scopes, the growing threat of personal legal liability in the wake of breaches, and inadequate budgets. A Cybersecurity Ventures report estimates there is only one CISO for every 10,000 businesses globally. With Gartner predicting that 50% of CISOs will also be responsible for disaster recovery by 2028, the report calls on corporate boards to treat cybersecurity as a fundamental leadership issue and invest in the role before a major incident occurs.
