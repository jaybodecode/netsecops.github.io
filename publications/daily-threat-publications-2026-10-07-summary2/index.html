FBI Warns of "FortiBleed" Campaign Locking Admins Out of Firewalls

The FBI and U.S. Secret Service issued a joint advisory on October 6, 2026, warning of the escalating "FortiBleed" campaign. This global operation targets Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPNs, not by exploiting a vulnerability, but by using leaked or brute-forced credentials. Attackers are using a custom tool to intercept credentials, crack them offline, and then lock legitimate administrators out of their own systems. The compromised access is then sold to ransomware affiliates, including the INC, Lynx, and Payload groups, posing a severe threat to critical infrastructure.

Oracle Health (Cerner) Breach Affects Nearly 20 Million Patients

The scale of a data breach involving legacy systems from Cerner, now part of Oracle Health, has been revised to affect nearly 20 million individuals. Disclosed in early October 2026, the incident stemmed from an attacker using compromised customer credentials to access servers between January and April 2026. The exposed data includes highly sensitive electronic protected health information (ePHI) such as names, Social Security numbers, and medical records, impacting patients across dozens of U.S. hospitals and triggering multiple class-action lawsuits.

South Korean Financial Firms Targeted with AI-Powered Pentest Tool

A new report from CrowdStrike reveals that a financially motivated threat actor has targeted South Korean financial organizations using ARTEX, an open-source agentic penetration testing tool. The campaign, which has been active since late September 2026, leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) like Claude to automate and accelerate attacks. This incident marks a significant development in adversarial tradecraft, demonstrating the practical application of AI to increase the speed and scale of cyberattacks against the financial sector.

UK Education Tech Firm Bromcom Suffers Single Sign-On Data Breach

Bromcom, a UK-based software provider for educational institutions, has notified customers of a data breach affecting a legacy single sign-on (SSO) system. According to a report on October 7, 2026, an unauthorized party accessed and exfiltrated data including email addresses and registration details. Bromcom has assured customers that its core Management Information System (MIS) containing sensitive student data was not affected, and no passwords were compromised.

ASOS Customers Receive 'Hacked' Messages After Partner Breach

Customers of the online fashion retailer ASOS received bizarre push notifications, including one reading "ASOS has been hacked," on October 6, 2026. The company confirmed the incident was caused by a security breach at a third-party customer communications platform. While customer names and contact details may have been exposed, ASOS stated that sensitive payment information and passwords are not believed to be compromised. The incident highlights the significant risks associated with supply chain partners.

FBI Investigates Breach of Jobs Portal; ShinyHunters Claims Data Theft

Update:The FBI has confirmed that the breach of its FBIJobs.gov portal was caused by an Accenture contractor's failure to apply a critical security patch for an Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability. This negligence allowed ShinyHunters to exfiltrate highly sensitive data, including counterintelligence workers' roles, home addresses of human intelligence operatives, and medical records of bureau employees. The FBI has since removed the contractor, highlighting significant supply chain security risks and the critical importance of timely patch management. This update clarifies the root cause of the incident, shifting from an initial claim of a zero-day to a preventable patching failure.

Europol Dismantles KillSec Ransomware; Teenager Suspected Leader

Update:Further details on Operation KillSwitch confirm the involvement of seven countries, including the US, UK, and Spain, where the teenage operator was apprehended. A deeper technical analysis reveals KillSec's primary modus operandi was scanning and exploiting public-facing cloud applications (T1190) for data exfiltration (T1530), often without deploying ransomware. This focus on data theft for extortion highlights a nuanced approach. New cyber observables and mitigation strategies are provided, emphasizing cloud security posture management, anomaly detection in cloud logs, and DLP for large egress from cloud storage.

Atlassian Patches Critical File Access Flaw in Jira and Confluence

Update:Reports indicate that CVE-2026-21589, the critical arbitrary file access vulnerability affecting Atlassian Data Center and Server products, is now being actively exploited in the wild. Exploitation attempts were observed merely hours after technical details and a Proof-of-Concept (PoC) were made public on October 6, 2026. This rapid weaponization underscores the heightened threat level and the critical need for organizations to apply available patches immediately to prevent potential compromise and data exposure.
