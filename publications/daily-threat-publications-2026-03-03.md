# Pentagon Blacklists AI Firm Anthropic, Android Patches Zero-Day, and Iran Conflict Ignites Cyber Front

**Published:** 2026-03-03 | **Articles:** 2

This period's cybersecurity landscape is marked by significant geopolitical and corporate policy shifts. The U.S. Pentagon has designated AI developer Anthropic a 'supply chain risk,' barring its use by federal agencies. Concurrently, Google has released a critical Android update patching an actively exploited zero-day vulnerability in Qualcomm chipsets. The escalating military conflict involving Iran has triggered a surge in hacktivism and state-sponsored cyber threats, while the University of Hawaiʻi confirmed a massive data breach from a 2025 ransomware attack, exposing the data of 1.2 million individuals. These events highlight the convergence of national security, technology policy, and cyber warfare.

## Articles in this publication
- [Google Patches Actively Exploited Qualcomm Zero-Day in Massive Android Update](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/google-patches-actively-exploited-qualcomm-zero-day-in-android/) (critical)
  Google's March 2026 security update for Android addresses 129 vulnerabilities, including a high-severity zero-day flaw, CVE-2026-21385, in a Qualcomm display component. The vulnerability, a memory corruption issue affecting over 230 Qualcomm chipsets, is confirmed to be under limited, targeted exploitation. The patch is included in the '2026-03-05' security patch level, and users are urged to update their devices as soon as the patch is made available by their respective manufacturers to mitigate the risk of compromise.
- [Pentagon Blacklists Anthropic AI, Citing National Security Risk Over Usage Policy Dispute](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/pentagon-designates-anthropic-supply-chain-risk-over-ai-policy/) (high)
  In an unprecedented move, the U.S. Pentagon has designated artificial intelligence company Anthropic a 'supply chain risk,' effectively banning its technology, including the Claude AI model, from all federal agencies. The decision, announced March 2, 2026, and directed by President Donald Trump, stems from a fundamental disagreement over Anthropic's acceptable use policy (AUP). The Pentagon sought unrestricted use of the AI for 'all lawful purposes,' while Anthropic refused to lift prohibitions against its use in autonomous weapons and mass surveillance, leading to a contract breakdown and the sweeping directive.
