# Microsoft's Massive Patch Tuesday, Windows Zero-Days, and Supply Chain Attacks Rock the Cybersecurity Landscape

**Published:** 2026-05-14 | **Articles:** 10

This week in cybersecurity, Microsoft released a massive Patch Tuesday addressing over 130 vulnerabilities, while separately, two new Windows zero-days were dropped publicly by a disgruntled researcher. The supply chain remains a key target, with the 'Mini Shai-Hulud' campaign compromising over 170 open-source packages and the Nitrogen ransomware group hitting electronics giant Foxconn. Other major incidents include a critical Linux kernel flaw 'Fragnesia' allowing root access, and the EU's NIS2 Directive imposing strict new liability rules on the logistics sector. These events highlight the increasing speed and sophistication of threats, from rapid vulnerability exploitation to novel attack techniques.

## Articles in this publication
- [Belgium's NIS2 Audit Deadline Arrives, Kicking Off EU-Wide Enforcement](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/belgium-nis2-cybersecurity-audit-deadline-arrives-pressuring-eu-entities/) (informational)
  April 18, 2026, marks a significant milestone in European cybersecurity regulation, as Belgium becomes the first EU member state to mandate the completion of an initial NIS2 directive conformity assessment. 'Essential entities' in sectors like energy, health, and transport must now have their cybersecurity posture audited by an accredited body. This move signals the beginning of active NIS2 enforcement across the bloc, putting pressure on organizations to comply with stringent new requirements. Key mandates include a 24-hour incident reporting window, personal liability for management, and documented proof of risk assessments and incident response plans, all of which will significantly impact Security Operations Centers (SOCs).
- [Anthropic's 'Mythos' AI Model Signals New Era of Autonomous Cyber Threats](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/anthropics-mythos-ai-model-sparks-autonomous-cyber-threat-fears/) (critical)
  AI research firm Anthropic has developed a frontier AI model, 'Claude Mythos Preview,' with capabilities so advanced that it has been deemed too dangerous for public release. The model can autonomously discover unknown zero-day vulnerabilities, generate functional exploits, and execute multi-stage cyberattacks, marking what some call a 'watershed moment for cybersecurity.' During testing, Mythos reportedly identified thousands of high-severity flaws. While Anthropic has restricted access, launching 'Project Glasswing' to allow vetted partners like Apple and Goldman Sachs to use the model for defensive purposes, concerns have been amplified by reports that unauthorized users may have briefly gained access to the model through a third-party contractor. This development accelerates the timeline for AI-driven attacks and heightens the urgency for developing AI-native defenses.
- [Instructure Confirms Massive Breach; ShinyHunters Claims 275 Million User Records from Canvas LMS](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/instructure-canvas-breach-shinyhunters-claims-275-million-records/) (high)
  Educational technology firm Instructure has confirmed a significant data breach affecting its widely used Canvas Learning Management System (LMS). The incident, which caused service disruptions starting May 1, 2026, has been claimed by the notorious extortion group ShinyHunters. The threat actor alleges the theft of 3.65 terabytes of data, including the personal information and private messages of 275 million students and educators from nearly 9,000 institutions globally. Instructure has acknowledged the exposure of names, email addresses, and user messages but stated that more sensitive data like passwords and financial information was not compromised. The company is working with law enforcement and has implemented remedial actions, including rotating API keys.
- [Iranian APT MuddyWater Masquerades as Ransomware Group in Microsoft Teams-Based Espionage Campaign](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/iranian-apt-muddywater-uses-microsoft-teams-for-false-flag-ransomware-attacks/) (high)
  The Iranian state-sponsored advanced persistent threat (APT) group MuddyWater, also known as Mango Sandstorm, is conducting a sophisticated espionage campaign disguised as a ransomware attack. First observed in early 2026, the operation uses social engineering via Microsoft Teams to trick victims into revealing credentials during interactive screen-sharing sessions, bypassing MFA. Instead of encrypting files, the attackers deploy remote management tools like DWAgent and AnyDesk to exfiltrate data and establish long-term persistence. The campaign, attributed to MuddyWater with moderate confidence, targets organizations of strategic interest to Iran across the U.S., Europe, APAC, and the Middle East, using criminal tactics and off-the-shelf tools to obscure its state-sponsored origins.
- [Foxconn Hit by Nitrogen Ransomware; Gang Claims Theft of Apple, Intel Data](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/foxconn-confirms-ransomware-attack-by-nitrogen-gang-on-us-factories/) (critical)
  Global electronics manufacturer Foxconn has confirmed a ransomware attack that disrupted operations at its North American factories. The Nitrogen ransomware gang, believed to be an offshoot of the Conti group, has claimed responsibility. The attackers allege they stole 8 terabytes of data, including sensitive project files related to Foxconn's high-profile clients like Apple, Intel, and Google, and have posted samples on their dark web leak site.
- [Microsoft's May Patch Tuesday: 137 Flaws Fixed, Including Critical Netlogon RCE](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/microsofts-may-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-137-vulnerabilities/) (high)
  Microsoft's May 2026 Patch Tuesday is one of the largest of the year, addressing 137 vulnerabilities. In a notable break from a 22-month trend, no actively exploited zero-day vulnerabilities were fixed. The update includes patches for 30 critical flaws, most notably a 9.8 CVSS RCE in the Windows Netlogon service (CVE-2026-41089) and another 9.8 CVSS RCE in the Windows DNS Client (CVE-2026-41096), both of which require immediate attention.
- [Novel 'GemStuffer' Campaign Abuses RubyGems Repository as Data Exfiltration Channel](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/gemstuffer-campaign-abuses-rubygems-repository-for-data-exfiltration/) (medium)
  A novel and unusual campaign, dubbed 'GemStuffer,' has been observed abusing the RubyGems package registry. Threat actors have published over 150 malicious gems that, instead of containing malware to infect developers, use the registry itself as a free and unconventional data storage and exfiltration channel. The scripts within the packages are designed to scrape web pages from U.K. local government portals, package the scraped HTML into valid gem archives, and then publish them back to the RubyGems repository using hardcoded API keys. The motive remains unclear, but the activity coincided with RubyGems temporarily disabling new account registrations due to a 'major malicious attack.'
- [Researcher Leaks Two Windows Zero-Day Exploits, 'YellowKey' and 'GreenPlasma', Amid Dispute with Microsoft](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/researcher-leaks-two-windows-zero-day-exploits-yellowkey-and-greenplasma/) (critical)
  A security researcher known as Chaotic Eclipse has publicly released proof-of-concept exploits for two new zero-day vulnerabilities in Microsoft Windows, which they have named 'YellowKey' and 'GreenPlasma.' The release is an escalation of an ongoing dispute between the researcher and the Microsoft Security Response Center (MSRC). YellowKey is reportedly a BitLocker encryption bypass, while GreenPlasma is a privilege escalation flaw. The vulnerabilities are allegedly being actively exploited in the wild within hours of the public disclosure, placing Windows users at immediate and significant risk.
- [SAP Patches Critical Flaws in Commerce Cloud and S/4HANA with 9.6 CVSS Score](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/sap-patches-critical-vulnerabilities-in-commerce-cloud-and-s4hana/) (critical)
  SAP has released its May 2026 Security Patch Day updates, addressing two critical vulnerabilities in its enterprise products that both carry a CVSS score of 9.6. The first flaw, CVE-2026-34263, is an arbitrary code execution vulnerability in SAP Commerce Cloud caused by an improper Spring Security configuration. The second, CVE-2026-34260, is a SQL injection vulnerability in the SAP Enterprise Search for ABAP component of S/4HANA. Both flaws could allow attackers to gain significant unauthorized access and control over affected systems, prompting an alert from Singapore's Cyber Security Agency (CSA).
- [PraisonAI Auth Bypass (CVE-2026-44338) Exploited Within Four Hours of Disclosure](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/praisonai-auth-bypass-cve-2026-44338-exploited-within-hours-of-disclosure/) (critical)
  A critical authentication bypass vulnerability in the PraisonAI framework (CVE-2026-44338) was actively exploited by attackers less than four hours after its public disclosure. The flaw, which stems from a legacy API server being shipped with authentication disabled by default, allows unauthenticated remote attackers to take control of AI agent workflows. Security firm Sysdig observed an automated scanner with the user agent 'CVE-Detector/1.0' probing for the vulnerability almost immediately after the advisory was published. This incident is a stark illustration of the rapidly shrinking window between vulnerability disclosure and mass exploitation in the age of automated attack tools.
