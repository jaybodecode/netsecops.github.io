Cyberattack on Refrigeration Systems Spoils Food at French Stores

A large-scale cyberattack targeting the refrigeration systems of hundreds of supermarkets in France, Belgium, and the Netherlands has resulted in millions of euros in food spoilage. Attackers remotely triggered forced defrost cycles, raising temperatures to 50°C. The incident is linked to compromised systems from Danish provider Danfoss, highlighting vulnerabilities in industrial control systems (ICS) within the food supply chain. French authorities have launched an investigation into the attack.

Citrix Discloses Critical RCE Flaw in NetScaler ADC and Gateway

Citrix has released a security advisory for a new critical vulnerability, CVE-2026-107406, affecting its NetScaler ADC and Gateway products. The flaw is a memory overflow issue with a CVSS score of 9.5 that could lead to remote code execution or denial of service. It impacts appliances configured as a SAML Service Provider or Identity Provider. CISA has urged customers to apply patches immediately, although there is no evidence of active exploitation yet.

Karaoke Giant Daiichi Kosho Supplier Breach Exposes 8.7M Customers

Japanese karaoke giant Daiichi Kosho has announced a major data breach originating from a third-party supplier, Nippon Columbia Group (NCG). The incident, caused by malware on a supplier employee's PC, may have exposed the personal information of approximately 8.7 million customers. The potentially leaked data includes names, birth dates, and contact information, but not financial details. This event highlights the significant risks posed by supply chain vulnerabilities.

Osaka University Hit by Ransomware; 500 Servers Affected

Osaka Metropolitan University in Japan has been hit by a major suspected ransomware attack, causing widespread operational disruption. The attack has impacted approximately 500 servers, affecting classes, email, and administrative systems. The university has also warned that the personal data of at least 130,000 students, faculty, and staff may have been compromised in the incident. This event highlights the ongoing trend of ransomware groups targeting the education sector.

Article Updates

Denmark's National Population Register Breached, 8.8M Records Exposed

Update:A new report from Check Point Research on October 12, 2026, reconfirms the catastrophic data breach of Denmark's Central Register of Persons (CPR). The incident, which exposed 8.8 million records including names, addresses, and national ID numbers, was caused by a compromise at a private third-party supplier. The report highlights that the number of exposed records exceeds Denmark's total population, likely due to the inclusion of historical data. The breach continues to pose a severe risk of identity theft and fraud, underscoring the critical vulnerability of trusted third-party relationships in national infrastructure.

US Seizes Hacking Tools Used by China-Linked Group Flax Typhoon

Update:A joint advisory from CISA, FBI, NSA, and allied nations has further highlighted the global threat posed by China-based Integrity Technology Group. The warning expands the identified target regions to include North America, Southeast Asia, and Africa, encompassing critical infrastructure, manufacturing, healthcare, and energy sectors. Additionally, the advisory details new tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) employed by threat actors like Flax Typhoon, including Living-off-the-Land (LotL) techniques and the use of compromised VPN infrastructure for persistence. The advisory also mentions 'Red Juliett' as another threat actor benefiting from Integrity Tech's tools, underscoring the widespread nature of these state-sponsored cyber operations.

Warlock Ransomware Hits Critical Sectors via Old SharePoint Flaws

Update:The Warlock ransomware group's campaign, exploiting 'ToolShell' SharePoint vulnerabilities, has now been confirmed to include at least four U.S. critical infrastructure organizations among its victims. This expands the previously reported geographical scope from Europe, Africa, and Latin America to include the United States, specifically targeting sectors such as water utilities and telecommunications providers. The focus on U.S. critical infrastructure highlights an increased threat to national security and essential services, even if the overall number of confirmed victims remains consistent.
