New Ransomware 'SAMURAI-SEC' Emerges from Chaos Family

HIGH

SAMURAI-SEC Ransomware Deploys Double-Extortion Tactics

A new ransomware variant, named 'SAMURAI-SEC,' has been identified by security researchers. The malware is derived from the builder of the Chaos ransomware family and employs a double-extortion strategy. It encrypts victims' files, appends a '.samurai' extension, and exfiltrates data to pressure them into paying a ransom. The ransom note threatens to publish the stolen data if the attackers are not contacted within 48 hours, highlighting the continued evolution of the Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) model.

BlackX: New UEFI Bootkit with BlackLotus Similarities Emerges

CRITICAL

BlackX UEFI Bootkit: A New Threat with Echoes of BlackLotus

Security researchers have discovered a new UEFI bootkit named 'BlackX' being used in a targeted APT campaign against diplomatic agencies in Central Asia. The bootkit is highly sophisticated, capable of bypassing the Secure Boot mechanism to achieve persistent and stealthy control over a compromised system. BlackX shares several characteristics with the infamous BlackLotus bootkit, indicating a high level of technical expertise by its creators and a worrying evolution in firmware-level threats.

ConnectWise Suspends Services After M365 Supply Chain Attack

CRITICAL

ConnectWise Cloud Services Hit by M365 Supply Chain Attack

Update:

Microsoft has officially attributed the ConnectWise Cloud Backup supply chain attack to Storm-0940, a China-linked advanced persistent threat (APT) group. The attack, which occurred between October 15th and 17th, 2026, leveraged a stolen application credential to gain unauthorized access to customer tenants in both Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace, expanding the previously known scope. Threat actors utilized automated tools, including an open-source AI penetration-testing tool named ARTEX, to rapidly authenticate against thousands of tenants. This attribution and new technical details highlight the sophisticated nature and broader impact of the incident.

LiteLLM Supply Chain Attack Steals Cloud and AI Service Keys

HIGH

LiteLLM Supply Chain Compromise on PyPI and Docker

Update:

Further analysis of the LiteLLM supply chain attack (version 1.107.0) confirms the presence of a sophisticated backdoor in addition to the infostealer. This backdoor allows threat actors to execute arbitrary commands on compromised developer machines, significantly increasing the risk beyond credential theft. The incident now maps to additional MITRE ATT&CK techniques including T1555.005 (Credentials from Password Stores), T1059.006 (Python execution), and T1048 (Exfiltration Over Alternative Medium). This expanded capability means attackers could pivot into corporate networks, inject malicious code into company software, and exfiltrate proprietary data, escalating the overall severity and potential for widespread organizational impact.

GitLab Patches Critical Integer Overflow Vulnerability

CRITICAL

GitLab Security Release Patches Critical Flaw

Update:

Further details have emerged regarding the critical GitLab vulnerability. It has been assigned CVE-2026-110264 with a CVSS score of 10.0, confirming it as an unauthenticated remote code execution (RCE) flaw. The vulnerability is rooted in the third-party Onigmo regular expression library used by Ruby. It affects all self-managed GitLab instances from version 7.0 up to 19.5.0, significantly broadening the scope of potentially vulnerable systems. Administrators are urged to upgrade to version 19.5.1 immediately, and to assume compromise if publicly exposed instances were not patched promptly.
