CISA Unveils "CISA 2030" for Proactive Cyber Defense

The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has released its "CISA 2030: Defend, Harden, Prevail" strategic plan. The new doctrine marks a major shift from a reactive defense posture to an 'aggressive, anticipatory stance' against threats to U.S. critical infrastructure. The strategy prioritizes focusing resources on the highest-risk systems and sectors, enhancing the security of operational technology (OT), and strengthening software supply chains. CISA aims to work more closely with the intelligence community and private sector to translate threat intelligence into faster operational actions, with aggressive internal deadlines for implementation.

Conduent Proposes $80M Settlement for Breach of 62M Records

Business services provider Conduent has proposed an $80 million settlement for a class-action lawsuit following a major data breach. The incident, which occurred between October 2024 and January 2025, exposed the personal and medical data of approximately 62 million individuals. The breach was notable for the prolonged attacker dwell time and the nine-month delay in notifying victims. The settlement fund aims to cover cash payments for documented losses, credit monitoring services, and legal fees for affected customers of Conduent's clients, which include major health insurers.

Navigate360 Breach Exposes SSNs After 3-Month Intrusion

Navigate360, a safety solutions provider for the education sector, has disclosed a data breach resulting from a prolonged network intrusion. An attacker had access to its systems for over three months, from November 2025 to March 2026, and exfiltrated files containing sensitive data like names and Social Security numbers. The company discovered the suspicious activity in March 2026 but only completed its investigation and began notifying victims seven months later, in October 2026. Affected individuals are being offered credit monitoring services.

Falcon Ransomware Claims Attack on Law Firm Weitz & Luxenberg

The Falcon ransomware group has claimed responsibility for a cyberattack against the U.S. national law firm Weitz & Luxenberg. The attack was reported on October 25, 2026. Details on the extent of the breach or the specific data exfiltrated are not yet public, but the incident highlights the ongoing trend of ransomware gangs targeting professional services firms, which hold highly sensitive and confidential client information. The attack likely involves a double-extortion tactic, where data is both encrypted and stolen for leverage.

Emperador Ransomware Claims Attack on US Firm A-RMS

The Emperador ransomware group has claimed a cyberattack on A-RMS, a U.S.-based organization. The claim was made on October 25, 2026. As is typical with such incidents, details are scarce, but the claim suggests that the group has breached the victim's network, encrypted files, and likely stolen data to use for extortion. This attack is another example of the persistent ransomware threat facing U.S. businesses. The scope of the breach and the nature of A-RMS's business are not yet publicly known.

Oracle's October 2026 CPU Fixes 1,283 Vulnerabilities, Some Critical

Update:New information regarding Oracle's October 2026 Critical Patch Update clarifies the scope, now reporting 1,295 patches for 1,209 unique vulnerabilities across 33 product families, including 95 critical flaws. Crucially, the update provides 'Cyber Observables' with hunting hints, such as monitoring default ports (1521, 7001, 3306), Oracle audit logs for suspicious activity, checking Java SE file versions, and identifying outdated versions via network banners. These details offer enhanced detection capabilities for organizations.

Critical 'PHP2Shell' Flaws Put 70% of Websites at Risk of RCE

Update:New information confirms the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities were actively exploited as zero-days, with Amazon Threat Intelligence observing attempts on October 17, 2026, four days before public announcement. The NSA has also issued an urgent advisory. CISA officially added both CVEs to its KEV catalog on October 26, 2026, mandating a patching deadline for federal agencies. The impact is more clearly defined, with major Japanese corporations (ANA, JR West, MUFG Bank) and government services in multiple European countries taking systems offline. Additional hunting hints and a new remediation step, application hardening by disabling unnecessary PHP functions, have been provided.

GitLab Patches Critical CVSS 10.0 RCE Flaw CVE-2026-110264

Update:Further analysis of CVE-2026-110264 reveals the critical unauthenticated RCE vulnerability stems from an integer overflow within a core component of GitLab, rather than the previously reported Onigmo regular expression library. This integer overflow leads to a memory corruption condition, allowing attackers to execute arbitrary code. While the CVSS 10.0 severity and impact remain unchanged, this clarification provides a more precise understanding of the flaw's technical root cause. No public exploitation has been observed, but rapid development of PoCs is anticipated. All self-managed instances still require an immediate upgrade to version 19.5.1.
