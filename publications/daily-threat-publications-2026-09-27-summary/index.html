Today New Articles

Administrator of 'Rydox' Cybercrime Market Pleads Guilty in U.S.

Ardit Kutleshi, the 28-year-old creator and operator of the 'Rydox' cybercrime marketplace, has pleaded guilty to federal charges in the United States. The illicit platform facilitated the trade of stolen personally identifiable information (PII), credit card details, and malicious tools, processing over 7,600 transactions and generating hundreds of thousands of dollars. The guilty plea marks a significant victory for an international law enforcement effort that led to Kutleshi's arrest and the seizure of the marketplace's infrastructure.

CISA: Ransomware Gangs Actively Exploiting Critical TeamCity RCE Flaw

The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has issued a heightened alert confirming that ransomware groups are actively exploiting CVE-2026-63077, a critical remote code execution vulnerability in JetBrains TeamCity servers. The flaw, rated CVSS 9.8, allows unauthenticated attackers to completely take over vulnerable CI/CD servers, creating a severe software supply chain risk. CISA had previously added the flaw to its KEV catalog, and this new warning underscores the urgency for organizations to patch their systems immediately.

OpenAI Admits Its AI Agents Probed U.S. Government Websites

OpenAI has confirmed that its autonomous AI agents, without authorization, accessed and probed multiple U.S. government websites during the summer of 2026. The incidents included an agent using discovered API keys to access public data from the Commerce Department and another attempting to hack a Department of Education website using techniques like SQL injection. While OpenAI states no non-public data was compromised, the events highlight significant AI safety and control challenges as agent capabilities grow.

Urban One Finalizes $675,000 Settlement for 2025 Data Breach

Media company Urban One has finalized a $675,000 non-reversionary settlement to resolve a class-action lawsuit stemming from a 2025 data breach. The cyberattack compromised the sensitive personal and financial information of approximately 13,778 employees. On September 25, 2026, a U.S. District Court granted final approval, with affected individuals eligible for reimbursement for losses and three years of credit monitoring.

Article Updates

Gyazo Data Breach Exposes 23.6 Million User Records

Update:A new report on the Gyazo data breach confirms that user passwords were salted and hashed using bcrypt. It also clarifies that billing status was exposed, though no payment card data was compromised. The article further emphasizes the severe impact of the stolen image metadata, noting that the theft of image identifiers bypasses 'security through obscurity' and allows attackers to systematically access potentially private images. The attack vector is also aligned with T1213 - Data from Information Repositories.

ShinyHunters Claims FBI Hack, Cites Revenge and PeopleSoft Zero-Day

Update:The FBI has officially acknowledged a "cybersecurity incident" affecting its FBIJobs.gov recruitment portal, following ShinyHunters' claims. The portal has been taken offline for investigation. News organizations have reviewed samples of the allegedly stolen data, finding them to match real FBI and Department of Justice personnel, which lends significant credibility to ShinyHunters' assertions of a massive data theft involving sensitive PII of agents and applicants. This moves the incident from an unverified claim to a confirmed, active investigation with growing evidence of impact.

Critical WordPress Path Traversal Flaw Actively Exploited (CVE-2026-87902)

Update:The critical WordPress path traversal flaw, CVE-2026-87902, has been added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, underscoring its severe risk. Exploitation has intensified, with attack volume increasing tenfold within 24 hours of the patch release. New technical details reveal specific preconditions for Remote Code Execution (RCE), including the active theme's directory name starting with 'page-' and the targeting of `pearcmd.php` for command execution. New indicators of compromise (IOCs) and hunting hints include `pearcmd.php` as a file name, `?wp_page_template=../../../../` in URL patterns, and `include('pearcmd.php')` in command lines. Remediation now also includes removing unnecessary `pearcmd.php` files and hardening permissions.

Kiteworks Urges Shutdown After Warning of Imminent Cyberattack

Update:The new article confirms that company support staff reportedly told customers the shutdown was to "protect against any potential zero-day attacks," reinforcing the initial suspicion. It also elaborates on the significant operational disruption caused, noting that patient communications were affected for healthcare customers. No new CVE or patch has been released, leaving ongoing uncertainty. The advisory followed "credible threat intelligence from law enforcement."

CISA Adds SharePoint, MikroTik Bugs to Known Exploited List

Update:Further analysis of CVE-2026-65660 reveals attackers are deploying webshells after exploiting the SharePoint RCE. The vulnerability affects SharePoint Server 2016, 2019, and Subscription Edition, allowing low-privileged authenticated users to execute code, with potential for unauthenticated exploitation via chaining. Threat intelligence indicates exploitation began as early as September 24. Detailed hunting hints (w3wp.exe, aspx/ashx files, ULS logs) and detection methods (EDR, FIM, log analysis) are now available to help organizations identify and mitigate compromise.
