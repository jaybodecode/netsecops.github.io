# AI-Powered Attacks Escalate; Critical Vulnerabilities and Supply Chain Risks Dominate

**Published:** 2026-10-22 | **Articles:** 9

This daily cybersecurity publication highlights a significant escalation in AI-driven cyber threats, alongside critical vulnerabilities and ongoing supply chain risks. Microsoft Threat Intelligence has attributed the ConnectWise Cloud Backup supply chain attack to Storm-0940, a China-linked threat actor, noting their use of a custom AI agent framework named 'ARTEX' to automate credential abuse and accelerate unauthorized access to Microsoft 365 environments.  Similarly, threat actor 'BUGSTER' is actively weaponizing jailbroken AI tools for credential theft and VoIP fraud, demonstrating increased operational efficiency and sophistication.

Critical vulnerabilities remain a major concern. The PHP2Shell vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619) are experiencing millions of active exploitation attempts, prompting a CISA emergency directive for federal agencies and precautionary system shutdowns globally. GitLab has also patched a critical integer overflow vulnerability in its Community and Enterprise Editions, urging immediate upgrades for self-managed installations.

Supply chain attacks continue to pose a widespread threat. The popular AI gateway tool LiteLLM has suffered its second supply chain attack, with a malicious version distributing malware to harvest cloud and AI service keys. Kroll's 2026 Data Breach Outlook report indicates that 69% of organizations experienced data breaches in the past year, with breaches originating from third-party technology providers having the widest impact.

New advisories warn of prolific ransomware and phishing campaigns. The SafePay ransomware group is reportedly exploiting five specific CVEs for initial access to critical infrastructure, with updated guidance emphasizing urgent patching. Users of the Ngrave hardware wallet have lost approximately $2.56 million due to a phishing attack that compromised a third-party notification service, leading users to a counterfeit website to steal recovery phrases.  Additionally, the IT firm Computer Task Group (CTG) has disclosed a data breach exposing sensitive personal data, including Social Security numbers and health information.

## Articles in this publication
- [ConnectWise Shuts Down Cloud Services After Supply Chain Attack](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/connectwise-supply-chain-attack-forces-cloud-service-shutdown-impacts-m365/) (critical)
  IT software provider ConnectWise has suspended its Cloud Backup, SaaS Security, and Cloud Management services following a significant supply-chain attack. The incident involved a compromised application credential for its multitenant OAuth application, which threat actors used to gain unauthorized, high-privilege access to the Microsoft 365 environments of its Managed Service Provider (MSP) partners and their customers. The attack bypassed user-based MFA, leading ConnectWise to take services offline and reset credentials.
- [Critical PHP2Shell RCE Flaws Leaked Ahead of Coordinated Patch](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/critical-php2shell-vulnerabilities-leaked-ahead-of-patch/) (critical)
  Two critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerabilities in PHP, collectively named "PHP2Shell," were prematurely disclosed before a scheduled patch release. The flaws, tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, affect a vast range of PHP versions from 5.4 onwards, placing a majority of the web at risk. Security researchers have reproduced the exploit, and Cloudflare has already detected initial attack attempts in the wild. Organizations are urged to prepare for immediate patching as soon as the official fixes are released to mitigate the threat of widespread server compromise.
- [Advisory Warns of Prolific SafePay Ransomware Targeting Critical Infrastructure](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/joint-advisory-warns-of-highly-active-safepay-ransomware/) (high)
  CISA, the FBI, and international partners issued a joint advisory on the SafePay ransomware group, one of the most active operations of 2026. Since August 2024, the group has compromised approximately 600 organizations, including critical infrastructure sectors, across the Americas, Europe, and Australia. The advisory details the gang's common TTPs, which include credential theft, exploiting known vulnerabilities, and phishing. Agencies urge organizations to implement MFA, patch vulnerabilities, and maintain offline backups to defend against this widespread threat.
- [LiteLLM Supply Chain Attack Steals Cloud and AI Service Keys](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/litellm-hit-by-supply-chain-attack-stealing-cloud-credentials/) (high)
  The popular AI gateway tool LiteLLM has suffered its second supply chain attack in a year. On October 21, 2026, a malicious version, v1.107.0, was published to the PyPI and Docker Hub registries. The compromised package contained malware designed to harvest sensitive credentials, including cloud logins, developer secrets, and API keys for AI services. All users who may have installed the malicious version are urged to rotate all credentials immediately.
- [$2.56M Stolen from Ngrave Wallet Users via Phishing Attack](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/ngrave-wallet-users-lose-2-5-million-in-phishing-attack/) (high)
  Users of the Ngrave hardware wallet have lost approximately $2.56 million in a sophisticated phishing attack. Threat actors compromised a third-party service used for the Ngrave LIQUID companion app's push notifications. They sent fake security alerts that directed users to a counterfeit website, tricking them into entering their 24-word recovery phrases. The stolen assets include Bitcoin, Ethereum, and other cryptocurrencies.
- [Kroll Report: 69% of Orgs Breached in Past Year, Most via Third Parties](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/kroll-report-finds-69-percent-of-orgs-breached-in-past-year/) (informational)
  Kroll's 2026 Data Breach Outlook report reveals that 69% of organizations experienced at least one data breach in the past year, with 34% suffering multiple incidents. The report highlights the growing threat of supply chain risk, finding that breaches originating from third-party technology providers had the widest impact, affecting 69% of all individuals impacted in Kroll's incident response cases. A notable disparity exists in risk perception, with IT leaders more concerned about vendor breaches than C-suite executives.
- [Computer Task Group Breach Exposes SSNs and Health Information](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/computer-task-group-data-breach-exposes-ssns-and-health-data/) (high)
  The IT and staffing firm Computer Task Group (CTG) has disclosed a data breach that exposed a toxic combination of sensitive personal data. According to the disclosure, the breach exposed names, Social Security numbers, financial account information, and health plan data. In response, a national class-action law firm has launched an investigation into potential data privacy claims on behalf of affected individuals.
- [GitLab Patches Critical Integer Overflow Vulnerability](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/gitlab-releases-emergency-patches-for-critical-integer-overflow-vuln/) (critical)
  GitLab has released emergency security updates for its Community (CE) and Enterprise (EE) Editions to address several vulnerabilities, including a critical-severity integer overflow in the regular expression compiler. The patches also fix a high-severity improper authentication issue. GitLab strongly advises all administrators of self-managed installations to upgrade to a patched version immediately to protect their environments from potential exploitation.
- [Actor 'BUGSTER' Uses AI for Credential Theft and VoIP Fraud](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/threat-actor-bugster-uses-ai-for-cybercrime-planning-and-execution/) (high)
  A Unit 42 investigation has uncovered a threat actor, tracked as 'BUGSTER', actively weaponizing jailbroken AI tools to plan and execute a range of cybercrimes. The actor utilizes the Hermes Agent framework with the DeepSeek V4 model to bypass safety controls, enabling malicious activities primarily targeting entities in India. Reported operations include conducting a brute-force campaign against a university's WordPress site, successfully exfiltrating a database containing over 5,200 student records from another university, planning VoIP fraud, and running an automated Wi-Fi password cracking pipeline. This case highlights a concerning trend where threat actors are adopting AI to enhance their operational efficiency and sophistication.
