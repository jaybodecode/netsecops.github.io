# Critical Vulnerabilities, Supply Chain Attacks Dominate Cybersecurity News

**Published:** 2026-10-24 | **Articles:** 8

**Critical Vulnerabilities Under Active Exploitation**

- **PHP2Shell Vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619)**: Exploitation of these critical RCE vulnerabilities in PHP was observed as early as October 17, 2026, prior to public disclosure. The flaws impact widely used platforms like WordPress, Drupal, and Magento, and unsupported PHP versions will not receive official patches, leaving legacy websites vulnerable.
- **GitLab Integer Overflow Vulnerability (CVE-2026-110264)**: This critical, unauthenticated RCE flaw with a CVSS score of 10.0 affects self-managed GitLab instances from version 7.0 up to 19.5.0. Administrators are urged to upgrade to version 19.5.1 immediately and assume compromise for unpatched, publicly exposed instances.

**Supply Chain and Emerging Threats**

- **ConnectWise Supply Chain Attack**: Microsoft has attributed the ConnectWise Cloud Backup supply chain attack to Storm-0940, a China-linked APT group. The attack, occurring between October 15th and 17th, 2026, leveraged a stolen application credential to access customer tenants in Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace, utilizing automated tools for rapid authentication.
- **LiteLLM Supply Chain Attack**: Further analysis confirms a sophisticated backdoor in LiteLLM version 1.107.0, allowing arbitrary command execution on compromised developer machines. This expands the risk beyond credential theft, enabling potential pivoting into corporate networks and exfiltration of proprietary data.
- **New Ransomware 'SAMURAI-SEC'**: A new ransomware variant, SAMURAI-SEC, derived from the Chaos ransomware family, has emerged. It employs a double-extortion strategy, encrypting files, appending a '.samurai' extension, and exfiltrating data to pressure victims into paying a ransom within 48 hours.
- **Qilin Ransomware Targets Castle Howard Estate**: The Qilin ransomware gang has claimed responsibility for an attack on the UK's Castle Howard Estate, listing the estate on its data leak site. This incident highlights the indiscriminate targeting of ransomware gangs across various sectors.
- **BlackX UEFI Bootkit Emerges**: A new UEFI bootkit named 'BlackX' has been discovered in a targeted APT campaign against diplomatic agencies. It bypasses Secure Boot for persistent and stealthy control, sharing characteristics with the BlackLotus bootkit and indicating an evolution in firmware-level threats.

## Articles in this publication
- [ConnectWise Suspends Services After M365 Supply Chain Attack](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/connectwise-suspends-cloud-services-after-m365-supply-chain-attack/) (critical)
  IT software provider ConnectWise suspended its Cloud Backup and other cloud services following a major supply chain attack. An attacker used a compromised API secret for the ConnectWise Cloud Backup service to gain unauthorized access to the Microsoft 365 tenants of hundreds of its customers. The breach allowed the threat actor to access sensitive data in Exchange, Teams, and SharePoint. ConnectWise has shut down the affected services to investigate, leaving customers unable to access backups or perform restorations for several days.
- [Critical RCE Vulnerabilities in PHP Affect Millions of Web Servers](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/critical-php-vulnerabilities-cve-2026-109618-cve-2026-109619/) (critical)
  Two critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, have been disclosed in PHP, a ubiquitous server-side scripting language. Both vulnerabilities carry a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.2, indicating a high risk of exploitation. According to Palo Alto Networks' Cortex Xpanse, over 22.6 million internet-exposed instances are potentially vulnerable. Proof-of-concept (PoC) exploit code is reportedly available, and at least one European government has taken its website offline as a precautionary measure. Immediate patching is strongly recommended for all organizations using PHP to prevent potential server compromise, data theft, or malware deployment.
- [Critical 'PHP2Shell' Flaws Put 70% of Websites at Risk of RCE](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/critical-php-vulnerabilities-trigger-global-patch-scramble/) (critical)
  A set of critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerabilities, dubbed 'PHP2Shell' and tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, have been disclosed in PHP's core engine. Discovered by Wiz Research, the flaws affect nearly every PHP version from the last decade, placing an estimated 70% of global websites at immediate risk of server takeover. The U.S. CISA has issued an emergency directive urging immediate patching, while security firms report observing millions of active exploitation attempts just hours after public disclosure.
- [LiteLLM Supply Chain Attack Steals Cloud and AI Service Keys](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/litellm-hit-by-supply-chain-attack-stealing-cloud-credentials/) (high)
  The popular AI gateway tool LiteLLM has suffered its second supply chain attack in a year. On October 21, 2026, a malicious version, v1.107.0, was published to the PyPI and Docker Hub registries. The compromised package contained malware designed to harvest sensitive credentials, including cloud logins, developer secrets, and API keys for AI services. All users who may have installed the malicious version are urged to rotate all credentials immediately.
- [GitLab Patches Critical Integer Overflow Vulnerability](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/gitlab-releases-emergency-patches-for-critical-integer-overflow-vuln/) (critical)
  GitLab has released emergency security updates for its Community (CE) and Enterprise (EE) Editions to address several vulnerabilities, including a critical-severity integer overflow in the regular expression compiler. The patches also fix a high-severity improper authentication issue. GitLab strongly advises all administrators of self-managed installations to upgrade to a patched version immediately to protect their environments from potential exploitation.
- [New Ransomware 'SAMURAI-SEC' Emerges from Chaos Family](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/samurai-sec-new-double-extortion-ransomware-emerges-from-chaos-family/) (high)
  A new ransomware variant, named 'SAMURAI-SEC,' has been identified by security researchers. The malware is derived from the builder of the Chaos ransomware family and employs a double-extortion strategy. It encrypts victims' files, appends a '.samurai' extension, and exfiltrates data to pressure them into paying a ransom. The ransom note threatens to publish the stolen data if the attackers are not contacted within 48 hours, highlighting the continued evolution of the Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) model.
- [Qilin Ransomware Claims Attack on UK's Castle Howard Estate](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/qilin-ransomware-gang-claims-cyberattack-on-castle-howard-estate/) (high)
  The notorious Qilin ransomware gang has claimed responsibility for a cyberattack on the Castle Howard Estate, a famous historical landmark in the United Kingdom. The group has listed the estate on its dark web data leak site, indicating that they have exfiltrated sensitive data and are using the threat of its release to extort a ransom. The incident highlights the indiscriminate nature of ransomware gangs, who increasingly target organizations across all sectors, including cultural and heritage sites.
- [BlackX: New UEFI Bootkit with BlackLotus Similarities Emerges](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/blackx-uefi-bootkit-a-new-persistent-threat-with-similarities-to-blacklotus/) (critical)
  Security researchers have discovered a new UEFI bootkit named 'BlackX' being used in a targeted APT campaign against diplomatic agencies in Central Asia. The bootkit is highly sophisticated, capable of bypassing the Secure Boot mechanism to achieve persistent and stealthy control over a compromised system. BlackX shares several characteristics with the infamous BlackLotus bootkit, indicating a high level of technical expertise by its creators and a worrying evolution in firmware-level threats.
