Today New Articles

Optimizely Supply-Chain Attack Injects Malicious Code into 178 Major Websites

A large-scale supply-chain attack compromised 178 organizations, including the BBC, CNN, and Zoom, by injecting malicious JavaScript via the Optimizely web experimentation platform. The malware, served from Optimizely's own CDN, was designed to skim payment card details, steal cryptocurrency, and deliver exploits to iPhones. The incident highlights the significant risks posed by third-party service compromises, allowing attackers to target visitors of trusted, high-traffic websites.

FBI Warns of ARTEX, an AI-Powered Tool for Autonomous Cyberattacks

The FBI has issued a FLASH advisory about ARTEX, an open-source, AI-driven penetration testing framework being weaponized by threat actors. The tool automates complex cyberattacks by translating natural language goals into machine-executable actions. It has been used in attacks against finance, government, and healthcare sectors globally, with some intrusions achieving privileged access in under three hours. The advisory signals a new era of autonomous, machine-speed attacks, lowering the barrier for less-skilled actors and accelerating campaigns.

Critical Apache Axis RCE Flaw Actively Exploited in N-able, Atlassian

A critical remote code execution (RCE) vulnerability, CVE-2026-90436, in the end-of-life Apache Axis library is being actively exploited in the wild. The flaw, rated CVSS 9.8, affects enterprise products that use the library, including Atlassian Confluence Data Center and N-able N-central. Security firm Rapid7 has confirmed exploitation, and with a proof-of-concept available, organizations are urged to apply vendor patches on an emergency basis to mitigate the immediate threat of system compromise.

Microsoft Warns of Phishing Campaign Impersonating M365 Notifications

Microsoft Threat Intelligence is tracking a high-volume credential phishing campaign that impersonates legitimate Microsoft 365 service health notifications. Detected on November 13, 2026, the emails use social engineering, creating a false sense of urgency by claiming a service outage has occurred and that messages will be deleted unless the user clicks a link to "restore" them. The campaign's primary goal is to steal user credentials for subsequent attacks.

Espionage Group TGR-STA-0049 Deploys Novel RATs with HTTP/3 C2

The cyberespionage group TGR-STA-0049 (also known as Earth Alux) is targeting government entities across Southeast Asia, South America, and Europe in a campaign active since at least July 2025. The threat actor uses two new custom RATs, PerfectPioneer and its successor KUKURAT, which employ the HTTP/3 protocol for stealthy command and control. Initial access is gained through exploiting vulnerable servers to deploy web shells and through secondary phishing attacks with tailored decoys.

Ransomware Gangs Qilin and Barracuda Claim New Corporate Victims

Update:The Qilin ransomware group, also known as 'Agenda', has claimed responsibility for a data breach at Strategic Properties, a US-based real estate organization. The company was listed on Qilin's dark web leak site on November 16, 2026. This incident further demonstrates the group's ongoing and prolific activity, continuing its double-extortion tactics against new targets. The attack underscores the persistent threat of RaaS operations and the need for robust cybersecurity measures.

Google Patches Eighth Actively Exploited Chrome Zero-Day This Year

Update:New information indicates that the actively exploited Chrome zero-day, CVE-2026-125350, also affects Android platforms, expanding the scope of vulnerable systems. The updated patch versions are now specified as 120.0.6099.109/110 for Windows and 120.0.6099.109 for Mac/Linux, differing from previously reported versions. Furthermore, the vulnerability is highly likely to be included in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, underscoring its critical nature and the urgency for immediate patching across all affected devices.

Stolen AI Key at Fireworks AI Triggers Supply Chain Attack on Cursor

Update:Further investigation into the Cursor supply chain attack reveals that Fireworks AI was compromised twice in October, leading to the theft of a specific Cursor access key. Attackers used this key to access a read-only Cursor storage bucket, where they found additional unredacted credentials, enabling privilege escalation within Cursor's environment. It was also clarified that Anthropic was not directly hacked, and Cursor has advised that simply uninstalling the malicious extension may not be sufficient for full remediation, indicating potential persistence.

Google Project Zero Ends 30-Day Grace Period for Disclosures

Update:Further details on Google Project Zero's updated disclosure policy reveal that the team explicitly cited the accelerated pace of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and exploitation as a primary driver for shortening deadlines. This rationale underscores a belief that the time between patch availability and active exploitation is now effectively zero. The policy shift has garnered mixed reactions within the security community. Notably, Dustin Childs from Trend Micro's Zero Day Initiative (ZDI) expressed concerns, suggesting that the drastically shorter deadlines could strain vendor resources, potentially leading to rushed or poorly tested patches, or an increase in vulnerabilities disclosed before a fix is ready. ZDI currently operates on a 120-day disclosure timeline.
