Google Patches Eighth Actively Exploited Chrome Zero-Day This Year

Google has released an emergency security update for Chrome to address a high-severity use-after-free vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-125350. The flaw, located in the XML component, is confirmed to be actively exploited in the wild, marking the eighth Chrome zero-day patched in 2026. The update, version 157.0.8103.50/.51, also fixes seven other high-severity issues. Users are urged to update their browsers immediately to mitigate the risk of arbitrary code execution.

Vanderbilt University Hit by 'BYOD' Ransomware, Over 1M Records at Risk

Vanderbilt University has confirmed a major cybersecurity incident, identified as a ransomware attack by the 'BYOD' group. The attackers claim to have stolen sensitive data, including Social Security numbers, for over one million students and staff. The university's IT department instructed campus members to disconnect from Wi-Fi as it works to contain the breach, which has disrupted campus systems and encrypted files with a '.byod' extension.

Ten Philippine Government Agencies Report Data Breaches

The Philippines' National Privacy Commission (NPC) has confirmed that ten government agencies, including the Department of Justice and the Department of Information and Communications Technology, have filed data breach notifications. The NPC is now assessing the scope and nature of the incidents, which raise serious concerns about the security of sensitive government-held data. The full extent of the breaches and the number of affected individuals have not yet been disclosed.

SAP Patches Four Critical Flaws with Maximum 10.0 CVSS Score

SAP's November Security Patch Day includes fixes for 35 vulnerabilities, four of which are rated with the maximum CVSS score of 10.0. These critical flaws, discovered by security firm Onapsis and named ICM2SHELL and PROXYGATE, could allow an unauthenticated attacker to achieve remote code execution on affected SAP NetWeaver, Web Dispatcher, and Gateway systems. While not yet exploited in the wild, a working exploit has been developed, and customers are urged to patch immediately.

Nitori Data Breach May Expose Records of 37.62 Million Customers

Japanese home furnishings giant Nitori Holdings has announced a major data breach that may have exposed the personal records of up to 37.62 million customers. The breach, which occurred in October due to unauthorized access, compromised customer email and home addresses. Nitori has confirmed that financial information like credit card details was not affected but is warning customers to be vigilant for phishing attempts.

CIRO Reaches $8.3M Settlement for 2025 Data Breach Affecting 750k

The Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) has agreed to a proposed $8.3 million national settlement for a class-action lawsuit following a 2025 data breach. The incident, which originated from a phishing attack, compromised the personal and financial data of approximately 750,000 Canadians, including Social Insurance Numbers and investment account details. The settlement, which still requires court approval, aims to compensate affected individuals.

Children's Foundation Data Breach Occurs via Third-Party IT Provider

The Children's Foundation has reported a data breach that occurred through a third-party IT service provider, Highland Shores Children's Aid Society. An unauthorized party accessed the provider's network in July, encrypted systems, and may have exposed the personal information of the foundation's volunteers, donors, and program applicants. The compromised data is limited to names and contact information; no financial data was affected.

Cyber-Physical Attack Hits 700 French Supermarkets via Refrigerators

Update:The French government has officially attributed the October cyber-physical attacks on supermarkets to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), specifically the 'Shahid Kaveh' unit (CyberAveng3rs). The attacks involved forcing 'abnormal, indefinite defrost cycles' and disabling temperature alarms, leading to at least 15 fires. A second wave of attacks was reported on November 2, expanding to other European countries and targeting additional critical sectors, including French blood banks. France is seeking EU sanctions and international arrest warrants against the responsible parties, escalating the incident's geopolitical impact.
