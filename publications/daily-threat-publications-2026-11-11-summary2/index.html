Microsoft Patches Actively Exploited Outlook Zero-Day in Massive Update

Microsoft's November 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed over 1,200 vulnerabilities, including a critical zero-day in Outlook, CVE-2026-124194, which is being actively exploited. The flaw allows attackers to steal NTLM authentication hashes by tricking users into opening a malicious message file. The vulnerability does not trigger in the Preview Pane, requiring direct user interaction. In response, CISA has added CVE-2026-124194 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, mandating federal agencies to patch by November 24, 2026.

N-able Takes N-central RMM Offline After 30+ Zero-Days Publicly Disclosed

IT software provider N-able was forced to take its hosted N-central RMM platform offline globally after a security researcher publicly disclosed over 30 zero-day vulnerabilities, dubbed 'Able2Shell.' The flaws, which include critical bugs leading to unauthenticated remote code execution, were published with proof-of-concept exploits. N-able has since released an emergency hotfix, 2026.4 HF2, and is advising all on-premises customers to patch immediately due to the severe supply chain risk posed to managed service providers (MSPs).

Blind Eagle APT Uses HTML Smuggling to Target Latin American Entities

The Spanish-speaking APT group Blind Eagle (APT-C-36) has launched a new espionage campaign targeting government, judicial, and financial entities in Latin America, with a focus on Colombia. The operation uses sophisticated spear-phishing emails with judicial themes that employ HTML smuggling to deliver a malicious ZIP archive, bypassing email security gateways. The final payload is a custom remote access trojan (RAT), derived from AsyncRAT, capable of data exfiltration and keystroke logging.

CISA Adds Six Actively Exploited Flaws to 'Must-Patch' KEV Catalog

On November 10, 2026, the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) added six new vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, indicating they are under active attack. The list includes critical flaws in products from Microsoft, Cisco, Barracuda, Google, and Openwave. Federal agencies are mandated to patch these vulnerabilities by specified deadlines, and CISA urges all organizations to prioritize their remediation to reduce exposure to active threats.

North Korean APT Kimsuky Linked to "Trigram" Espionage Campaign

A cyber-espionage operation named the 'Trigram' campaign has been attributed to the North Korean threat group Kimsuky (APT-43). The campaign targets organizations in the AI, blockchain, and logistics sectors in Europe and the U.S. Attackers use social engineering, primarily fake job offers, to lure software developers into a multi-stage infection process that delivers an information-stealing malware. The operation highlights North Korea's persistent focus on intelligence gathering and revenue generation from technology sectors.

Vanderbilt University Hit by 'BYOD' Ransomware, Over 1M Records at Risk

Update:Vanderbilt University has started restoring key services like Wi-Fi, email, and Okta following the BYOD ransomware attack that began on November 9, 2026. The BYOD group claimed responsibility on November 10, alleging massive data theft, including over one million SSNs, and a successful takeover of a domain controller, indicating deep network compromise. The university is investigating the extent of the breach while working to bring systems back online. An unusual audio message was also reported as part of the compromise.
