# Critical Exploits, Supply Chain Attacks, and New Vulnerabilities Highlight Cybersecurity Landscape

**Published:** 2026-10-12 | **Articles:** 6

This daily summary covers critical cybersecurity incidents and advisories from October 12, 2026. The landscape features active exploitation of network devices, sophisticated supply chain attacks, and newly disclosed vulnerabilities.

**Critical Vulnerabilities Under Active Exploitation**:

*   **Cisco and SonicWall Flaws Under Active Exploit; CISA Issues KEVs**: SonicWall SMA 1000 SSRF (CVE-2026-102255) is being actively exploited just days after patches were released. Researchers observed numerous exploit attempts originating from U.S. IP addresses, with the flaw allowing for full device takeover and remote code execution. Hundreds of appliances remain exposed, and immediate patching is urged for affected versions.
*   **New Critical RCE Flaw Found in Patched Citrix NetScaler Appliances**: Citrix has disclosed CVE-2026-107406, a critical memory overflow vulnerability in NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances, rated 9.5 CVSS. This flaw enables unauthenticated remote code execution and affects recent versions patched in September 2026. Exposure is contingent on the appliance's configuration as a SAML identity provider, and immediate patching is recommended.

## Articles in this publication
- [Iranian Actor Targets Iraqi Infrastructure with "Blinder Tunnel"](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/iranian-blinder-tunnel-campaign-targets-iraqi-critical-infrastructure/) (high)
  Unit 42 has detailed a sophisticated cyber-espionage campaign, named "Blinder Tunnel," orchestrated by an Iranian state-aligned threat actor (CL-STA-1178). Active since at least March 2026, the campaign targets critical infrastructure in Iraq by impersonating the Dubai Airports IT department in a fake recruitment process. Attackers lure targets, primarily software engineers, into downloading a trojanized coding challenge. This initiates a multi-stage infection chain deploying custom malware called ShelbyLoader V2. The operation is notable for its use of the GitHub API for command-and-control (C2) communications to evade detection, a technique known as living-off-the-cloud. The actor also employs emerging evasion techniques like AppDomainManager hijacking. Analysis revealed operational security failures by the attackers, which allowed researchers to link this activity to another campaign targeting an Israeli entity.
- [Cisco and SonicWall Flaws Under Active Exploit; CISA Issues KEVs](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/cisco-and-sonicwall-zero-day-vulnerabilities-actively-exploited/) (critical)
  Cisco and SonicWall are patching critical, actively exploited vulnerabilities in widely deployed enterprise products. Cisco fixed an authentication bypass (CVE-2026-76504, CVSS 9.8) in its Catalyst SD-WAN Manager. SonicWall patched a maximum-severity SSRF bug (CVE-2026-102255, CVSS 10.0) in its SMA 1000 series appliances. CISA has added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, mandating urgent patching for federal agencies.
- [Warlock Ransomware Hits Critical Sectors via Old SharePoint Flaws](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/warlock-ransomware-exploits-sharepoint-flaws-to-breach-critical-infrastructure/) (high)
  The China-linked Warlock ransomware group has breached at least four organizations by exploiting a year-old set of Microsoft SharePoint vulnerabilities known as 'ToolShell'. The campaign, active in the two months preceding October 1, 2026, targeted a water utility, a telecom provider, a government body, and a university across Europe, Africa, and Latin America. After gaining access via unpatched on-premises SharePoint servers, the attackers deploy web shells and use 'bring your own vulnerable driver' (BYOVD) techniques to disable security software before deploying the Warlock ransomware across the network.
- [Ledger Wallets Hit by Supply Chain Attack; Over $90M in Crypto Stolen](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/ledger-hardware-wallet-supply-chain-attack-leads-to-90-million-crypto-theft/) (critical)
  A sophisticated supply chain attack targeting Ledger hardware wallets has resulted in the theft of over $92 million in cryptocurrency from 311 wallets. The attack involved physical tampering with devices sold through CryptoBilis, an authorized reseller in Southeast Asia. Ledger confirmed that at least one compromised device contained an unauthorized hardware implant. This method bypasses the core security model of hardware wallets by compromising the device before it reaches the customer. Ledger has advised customers who purchased from the reseller to cease using the devices and transfer funds immediately.
- [iRhythm Notifies Over 360,000 Patients of Data Breach from June](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/irhythm-technologies-notifies-360000-patients-of-june-data-breach/) (high)
  Medical device maker iRhythm Technologies is notifying over 360,000 patients that their personal and medical data was stolen in a data breach that occurred in June 2026. The incident involved unauthorized access to business applications hosted by a third party. Attackers accessed and exfiltrated data between June 3 and June 8, then contacted iRhythm on June 9 with a ransom demand. The compromised data includes patient names, addresses, dates of birth, insurance information, and device serial numbers. Notifications to affected individuals began on October 2, 2026, nearly four months after the breach.
- [New Critical RCE Flaw Found in Patched Citrix NetScaler Appliances](https://cyber.netsecops.io/articles/critical-rce-flaw-cve-2026-107406-found-in-patched-citrix-netscaler-appliances/) (critical)
  Citrix has disclosed another critical vulnerability, CVE-2026-107406, in its NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances, rated 9.5 CVSS. This memory overflow flaw can lead to unauthenticated remote code execution (RCE). Alarmingly, the vulnerability affects not only older builds but also recent versions that were patched in September 2026 to fix other zero-day flaws. Exposure depends on the appliance's configuration as a SAML identity provider (IdP). While there are no known exploits in the wild, Citrix urges customers to apply the new patches immediately to prevent compromise.
