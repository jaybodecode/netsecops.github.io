# Coles Group Investigates Cyber Incident, No Data Loss Confirmed

**Severity:** medium | **Category:** Cyberattack,Incident Response,Security Operations | **Updated:** 2026-11-16 | **Reading time:** 3 min

Australian supermarket giant Coles Group has announced it is investigating a cybersecurity incident. In a statement to the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX) on November 16, 2026, the company confirmed its retail and online operations remain unaffected. Crucially, Coles stated that its investigation has so far found no evidence that customer, employee, or supplier data has been compromised or exfiltrated. The company is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC).

## Executive Summary
**[Coles Group Limited](https://www.coles.com.au/)**, a major Australian supermarket chain, disclosed on November 16, 2026, that it is actively investigating a cybersecurity incident. In a public announcement to the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX), the company stated that its investigation has, to date, found no evidence of compromised or exfiltrated data belonging to customers, staff, or suppliers. Store and online operations are continuing normally. Coles is collaborating with the **[Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC)](https://www.cyber.gov.au/)** and other relevant agencies. The early disclosure and current findings suggest the incident may have been detected and contained before significant damage occurred.

## Incident Timeline
- **November 16, 2026**: Coles Group Limited issues an announcement to the ASX, publicly disclosing the cybersecurity incident and the ongoing investigation.

## Response Actions
Coles Group has taken several key steps in response to the incident:
1.  **Investigation**: An internal investigation was immediately launched to determine the nature and scope of the incident.
2.  **Collaboration**: The company is working closely with the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) and has pledged to engage with other regulators and agencies as required.
3.  **Public Communication**: Coles proactively informed investors and the public via an official ASX announcement, maintaining transparency while the investigation is in its early stages.
4.  **Business Continuity**: The company has confirmed that its core retail operations, both in-store and online, have not been disrupted by the incident.

---

## Technical Findings
The public statement from Coles is limited in technical detail, which is typical for an ongoing investigation. The assertion that "no evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been compromised or removed" could imply several scenarios:
- **Early Detection**: The company's security systems may have detected the intrusion at an early stage, allowing for rapid containment before the attacker could access or exfiltrate data.
- **Disruptive, Non-Theft Attack**: The incident may have been a denial-of-service attack or another form of disruption that did not involve data theft.
- **Incomplete Investigation**: The investigation is still in progress, and while no evidence has been found *yet*, the possibility of a data compromise has not been entirely ruled out.

---

## Lessons Learned
This incident highlights the importance of a mature incident response plan.
- **Proactive Communication**: Coles' decision to issue a prompt, albeit limited, public statement demonstrates a commitment to transparency, which can help manage public perception and stakeholder confidence.
- **Collaboration with Authorities**: Engaging with national cybersecurity bodies like the ACSC provides access to additional resources, intelligence, and expertise, strengthening the response effort.
- **Resilience**: The fact that retail operations remain unaffected indicates good business continuity planning and potentially a segmented network architecture that isolates corporate IT from operational technology (OT).

---

## Mitigation Recommendations
While details are sparse, general recommendations for retail organizations to prevent and mitigate similar incidents include:
1.  **Network Segmentation**: Isolate point-of-sale (POS) systems, e-commerce platforms, and corporate networks from each other to prevent an intrusion in one area from spreading to others. This aligns with MITRE mitigation [`M1030 - Network Segmentation`](https://attack.mitre.org/mitigations/M1030/).
2.  **Endpoint Security**: Deploy advanced EDR solutions on all endpoints, including corporate workstations and servers, to detect and block malicious activity.
3.  **Third-Party Security**: Retailers rely heavily on suppliers. Implement a third-party risk management program to vet the security of suppliers and partners with access to your network or data.
4.  **Regular Audits and Testing**: Conduct regular penetration tests and security audits to identify and remediate vulnerabilities before they can be exploited.
