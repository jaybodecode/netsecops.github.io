# Uber Fined €825M by Dutch Regulator Over Automated Driver Deactivations

**Severity:** high | **Category:** Regulatory,Policy and Compliance | **Updated:** 2026-08-24 | **Reading time:** 4 min

The Dutch Data Protection Authority has fined Uber €825 million for violating Article 22 of the GDPR. The regulator found that between 2018 and 2022, Uber used fully automated systems to suspend and deactivate driver accounts based on suspected fraud or low ratings without meaningful human review. This is the second-largest GDPR fine ever issued. Uber has stated the practices are no longer in use and plans to appeal the 'disproportionate' fine.

## Executive Summary

The **[Dutch Data Protection Authority](https://autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en)** (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) has imposed a landmark €825 million (approximately $966 million) fine on **[Uber](https://www.uber.com)** for violating the **[General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation)**. The penalty, one of the largest in GDPR history, targets Uber's use of automated systems to suspend and deactivate driver accounts between 2018 and 2022. The regulator ruled that these decisions, which had significant financial consequences for drivers, were made without the required human oversight and transparency mandated by the regulation. Uber has announced its intention to appeal the fine, calling it disproportionate and stating that the practices have since been changed.

---

## Regulatory Details

The core of the violation centers on **Article 22 of the GDPR**, which grants individuals the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing if that decision produces legal or similarly significant effects. The Dutch DPA found that Uber's system for automatically flagging and deactivating drivers for suspected fraud (e.g., taking inefficient routes) or for receiving persistently low customer ratings fell into this category.

The regulator determined that these automated deactivations, which effectively terminated a driver's source of income, were made without meaningful human review. This lack of human intervention, coupled with a failure to adequately inform drivers about the automated decision-making process, constituted a "serious violation" of the GDPR. The case originated from a complaint by a French driver and was later expanded to include 170 others, with the Dutch authority taking jurisdiction as Uber's European headquarters is in Amsterdam.

---

## Affected Organizations

The primary organization affected is **Uber B.V.**, the company's Dutch subsidiary, and its parent, **Uber Technologies, Inc.** The decision directly impacts Uber's operational and compliance frameworks within the European Union. The ruling also sets a significant precedent for other gig economy platforms and companies that rely heavily on automated decision-making algorithms to manage their workforce.

---

## Compliance Requirements

This ruling underscores several key compliance requirements under the GDPR for any organization using automated decision-making systems:

1.  **Human-in-the-Loop:** For decisions with significant legal or financial impact on individuals, a purely automated process is generally prohibited. Organizations must implement meaningful human review before a final decision is made.
2.  **Transparency:** Data subjects must be clearly informed about the existence of automated decision-making, the logic involved, and the potential consequences.
3.  **Right to Contest:** Individuals must have a clear and accessible way to challenge the automated decision and request human intervention.

## Implementation Timeline

-   **2018 - 2022:** Period during which Uber's illegal automated practices were in effect.
-   **2023:** A €1.2 billion fine against Meta sets the record for the largest GDPR penalty.
-   **August 2026:** The Dutch Data Protection Authority announces the €825 million fine against Uber.
-   **Post-August 2026:** Uber is expected to file a formal appeal against the decision.

---

## Impact Assessment

The financial impact on Uber is immediate and substantial, representing the third and largest fine from the Dutch regulator. Reputational damage is also significant, reinforcing public concerns about the fairness of algorithmic management in the gig economy. This ruling will likely force a broad re-evaluation of AI and automated systems across many industries, pushing companies to invest more in compliance, transparency, and human oversight to avoid similar penalties. For drivers, the ruling validates long-held grievances about being unfairly managed by opaque algorithms.

---

## Enforcement & Penalties

The €825 million fine is the primary enforcement action. This penalty is second only to the €1.2 billion fine levied against Meta for data transfer violations, highlighting the willingness of European regulators to issue severe penalties for major GDPR infringements. The size of the fine is intended to be a powerful deterrent, signaling that the economic benefits of non-compliant automation will not outweigh the potential regulatory costs.

---

## Compliance Guidance

To avoid similar violations, organizations should:

1.  **Map Automated Decisions:** Conduct a thorough inventory of all automated decision-making processes and classify them based on their potential impact on individuals.
2.  **Implement Human Review Workflows:** For any high-impact decisions, design and implement a mandatory human review step. This review must be substantive and not merely a rubber-stamping exercise.
3.  **Update Privacy Notices:** Clearly and simply explain in privacy policies how and why automated decision-making is used, what logic it follows, and what the consequences are for the individual.
4.  **Establish Appeal Mechanisms:** Create a straightforward process for individuals to challenge automated decisions and obtain a review by a human.
5.  **Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs):** Before deploying new automated systems, conduct a DPIA to identify and mitigate privacy risks, particularly those related to Article 22.
