# Dutch Regulator Warns Financial Firms of Growing AI and Cyber Risks

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Regulatory,Policy and Compliance,Cloud Security | **Updated:** 2026-11-09 | **Reading time:** 4 min

The Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets (AFM) has issued a report warning that financial service providers in the Netherlands face increasing cyber risks from the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) and heavy reliance on third-party IT services. Published on November 9, 2026, the report calls for firms to implement responsible AI policies and improve IT risk management, stressing that companies retain ultimate responsibility for data protection even when services are outsourced.

## Executive Summary
On November 9, 2026, the **Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets (AFM)**, the Netherlands' financial conduct regulator, published a report detailing growing IT-related risks within the financial services sector. The report highlights two primary areas of concern: the rapid adoption of **[Artificial Intelligence (AI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence)** and the pervasive reliance on outsourced, third-party IT providers. The AFM is urging financial firms to bolster their cyber resilience by establishing formal policies for responsible AI use and enhancing their oversight of IT supply chains. The regulator reiterated that accountability for data protection and sound business operations cannot be outsourced and remains with the financial institution.

## Regulatory Details
The AFM's report is based on findings from early November 2026 and serves as a supervisory update for the Dutch financial sector. The key concerns and directives include:

### Responsible AI Adoption
The AFM acknowledges the competitive pressure to adopt AI but warns that it must be done responsibly. It calls on firms to develop and implement clear policies that address:
- **Data Reliability**: Ensuring the data used to train and operate AI models is accurate and unbiased.
- **Client Transparency**: Being transparent with clients about when and how AI is being used in service delivery.
- **Employee Knowledge**: Ensuring employees understand the capabilities and limitations of the AI tools they use.

### Third-Party IT Risk Management
The report expresses significant concern over the common practice of outsourcing critical IT functions, often to a small number of large providers. The AFM noted that many financial firms have limited internal IT security staff, making effective vendor oversight challenging. The regulator emphasized that under Dutch law, including the **Act on the Financial Supervision (Wet op het financieel toezicht)**, financial firms are legally obligated to manage their operations to ensure controlled and sound business practices. This obligation extends to outsourced services. Firms must be able to demonstrate that they are actively monitoring their IT providers and ensuring those providers comply with all relevant regulations, such as the **[GDPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation)**.

## Affected Organizations
The AFM's guidance applies to all financial service providers operating under its supervision in the Netherlands. This includes banks, investment firms, insurance companies, and other financial institutions. The report's findings are particularly relevant for small to medium-sized firms that may lack the resources for a dedicated, in-house cybersecurity team and are therefore more reliant on third-party vendors.

## Impact Assessment
The report signals a heightened focus from the AFM on cybersecurity and operational resilience. Financial firms in the Netherlands can expect increased scrutiny of their IT risk management frameworks, particularly concerning AI governance and vendor management. Failure to demonstrate adequate controls could lead to regulatory intervention, enforcement actions, and potential fines. The report also serves as a warning about the systemic risk posed by concentration in the IT provider market; a significant incident at a major cloud or software provider could have cascading effects across the entire financial sector. This aligns with the principles of forthcoming regulations like the EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), which places stringent requirements on managing third-party ICT risk.

## Compliance Guidance
Financial service providers in the Netherlands should take the following steps in response to the AFM's report:

1.  **Conduct an AI Risk Assessment**: Inventory all current and planned uses of AI within the organization. Assess the risks associated with each use case, focusing on data privacy, model accuracy, and ethical implications.
2.  **Develop an AI Governance Framework**: Formalize policies for the development, procurement, and deployment of AI systems, incorporating the AFM's recommendations on data reliability, transparency, and employee training.
3.  **Review Third-Party Contracts**: Re-evaluate all contracts with IT service providers. Ensure they include robust security requirements, rights to audit, and clear liability clauses for security incidents.
4.  **Enhance Vendor Due Diligence**: Implement a more rigorous due diligence process for new IT vendors and a continuous monitoring program for existing ones. This should include reviewing their security certifications (e.g., SOC 2, ISO 27001) and incident response capabilities.
