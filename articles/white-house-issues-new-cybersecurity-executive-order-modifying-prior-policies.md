# New White House Executive Order Modifies Cybersecurity Mandates

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Regulatory | **Updated:** 2026-11-23 | **Reading time:** 4 min

The White House has issued a new executive order, 'Sustaining Select Efforts to Strengthen the Nation's Cybersecurity,' which amends certain policies from previous directives. The new order removes a specific mandate for federal agencies to implement phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (MFA), although the requirement remains part of the broader Federal Zero Trust Strategy. It also alters software supply chain validation requirements for government contractors, shifting from upfront artifact submission to a post-incident enforcement model. The order signals a continued, but adjusted, focus on national cybersecurity priorities.

## Executive Summary
The White House has released a new executive order (EO) titled "Sustaining Select Efforts to Strengthen the Nation's Cybersecurity." This EO modifies and extends certain cybersecurity policies established by the prior administration, indicating a continued bipartisan commitment to strengthening national digital infrastructure, albeit with some adjustments to implementation and enforcement. Key changes include the removal of a specific deadline for federal agencies to adopt phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication (MFA) and a shift in how software supply chain security is validated for federal contractors. The order also extends timelines for certain initiatives managed by **[NIST](https://www.nist.gov/)** and **[CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/)**.

---

## Regulatory Details
The new executive order introduces several significant modifications to the U.S. government's cybersecurity posture and requirements for its partners.

### Phishing-Resistant MFA Mandate
A notable change is the deletion of a directive from a January 2025 EO that had set a firm deadline for federal agencies to implement phishing-resistant MFA. It is critical to understand that this does *not* eliminate the requirement itself. The mandate for phishing-resistant MFA is still a core component of the broader Federal Zero Trust Strategy outlined in OMB M-22-09. The removal of the specific EO language likely provides agencies with more flexibility on implementation timelines rather than rescinding the policy goal.

### Software Supply Chain Security
The EO also adjusts the software self-attestation requirements for federal contractors. Previously, software providers were required to produce specific validation artifacts and documentation alongside their self-attestation forms (confirming they follow secure development practices). The new order removes this upfront requirement, reverting to an earlier standard. Federal contractors must still self-attest to using secure software development practices, but enforcement and requests for evidence will now primarily occur *after* a potential security issue is identified, rather than as a prerequisite for procurement.

### Extended Timelines
The order extends the deadline for a pilot program by NIST and CISA to develop a "rules-as-code" system for cybersecurity policies. The new timeline is one year, extended from the original 180 days.

## Affected Organizations
*   **U.S. Federal Government Agencies:** These organizations are directly affected by the changes in implementation timelines for MFA and other security controls.
*   **Government Contractors:** Companies that provide software and services to the federal government are impacted by the revised software supply chain attestation process. The change may reduce the initial administrative burden but shifts the focus to being prepared for post-incident audits.

## Compliance Requirements
While some specific directives have been altered, the core compliance requirements remain largely intact, driven by existing policy documents:
*   **Zero Trust Architecture:** Federal agencies must continue their transition to a zero trust architecture as mandated by OMB M-22-09.
*   **Phishing-Resistant MFA:** This remains a key pillar of the Zero Trust strategy, and agencies are still expected to plan for and implement it.
*   **Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF):** Software providers to the government must still align with the principles of NIST's SSDF (SP 800-218) and self-attest to their practices.

## Impact Assessment
The new EO represents a subtle but important shift in federal cybersecurity strategy.
*   **For Federal Agencies:** The extended timelines and removal of specific EO mandates may provide welcome flexibility but could also lead to slower adoption of critical controls if not managed carefully. The reliance on the underlying Zero Trust strategy means the ultimate security goals have not changed.
*   **For Government Contractors:** The shift on software attestation may lower the barrier to entry for some smaller contractors by reducing upfront paperwork. However, it places a greater emphasis on maintaining robust, auditable records of secure development practices, as scrutiny will be higher following any security incident. This moves the model from "trust but verify upfront" to "trust, then verify when necessary."

## Enforcement & Penalties
Enforcement for software attestation will become more reactive. If a vulnerability or breach is traced back to a software provider's product, that provider can expect intense scrutiny of their self-attested practices. False attestations could lead to penalties under the False Claims Act, contract termination, and significant reputational damage.

## Compliance Guidance
*   **Federal Agencies:** Continue to prioritize the implementation of a Zero Trust strategy. Use the revised timelines to ensure a more thorough and sustainable rollout of controls like phishing-resistant MFA, rather than delaying the initiatives.
*   **Government Contractors:** Do not interpret the change in attestation as a relaxation of security standards. Instead, focus on embedding the NIST SSDF into your development lifecycle and maintaining detailed documentation. Be prepared to produce evidence of your secure practices on demand. Automate evidence collection where possible to streamline responses to future audits.
