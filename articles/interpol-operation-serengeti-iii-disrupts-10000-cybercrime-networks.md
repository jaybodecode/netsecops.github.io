# INTERPOL Disrupts 10,909 Malicious Networks, Arrests 1,462

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Threat Actor,Security Operations,Policy and Compliance | **Updated:** 2026-10-23 | **Reading time:** 3 min

A global law enforcement action led by INTERPOL, codenamed Operation Serengeti III, has resulted in the disruption of 10,909 malicious networks and the arrest of 1,462 suspects across 21 countries. The operation, which ran from June to August 2026, targeted a wide range of cybercrime activities and successfully recovered over $105 million in illicit funds. The total financial losses linked to the dismantled networks are estimated to exceed $385 million.

## Executive Summary
On October 22, 2026, **[INTERPOL](https://www.interpol.int/)** announced the successful conclusion of Operation Serengeti III, a massive international cybercrime crackdown. The operation, involving law enforcement from 21 countries between June and August 2026, led to 1,462 arrests and the takedown of 10,909 malicious networks. Authorities recovered over $105 million in stolen funds, with the total criminal enterprise estimated to have caused losses of over $385 million to 65,929 victims worldwide. The operation underscores the effectiveness of coordinated international efforts in combating borderless cyber threats.

## Response Actions
Operation Serengeti III was a coordinated effort involving police forces, financial intelligence units, and cybersecurity agencies from 21 participating nations. The response actions included:
- **Intelligence Sharing:** INTERPOL's Cybercrime Directorate facilitated the exchange of intelligence on malicious infrastructure, suspect identities, and financial trails.
- **Network Takedowns:** Law enforcement agencies worked with internet service providers (ISPs) and domain registrars to take down malicious servers, domains, and botnets.
- **Arrests:** Coordinated police actions were carried out to arrest the individuals responsible for operating the criminal networks.
- **Asset Recovery:** Financial investigators traced and seized illicitly obtained funds, leading to the recovery of over $105 million.

## Technical Findings
While specific technical details of the dismantled networks were not provided, they likely encompassed a wide range of cybercrime infrastructure, including:
- **Command and Control (C2) Servers:** Used to manage botnets and control malware-infected computers.
- **Phishing Infrastructure:** Websites and servers used to host fake login pages to steal credentials.
- **Malware Distribution Networks:** Systems used to host and spread malicious software like ransomware and information stealers.
- **Fraudulent E-commerce Sites:** Fake online shops designed to steal money and payment card information.
- **Money Laundering Networks:** Systems used to obscure and transfer illicit funds, often involving cryptocurrency.

---

## Lessons Learned
- **International Cooperation is Key:** As stated by INTERPOL's director of cybercrime, the borderless nature of cybercrime makes international cooperation the most effective tool for disruption. No single country can effectively combat global cybercrime alone.
- **Following the Money Works:** The recovery of $105 million demonstrates that focusing on the financial aspects of cybercrime is a powerful strategy. Disrupting money laundering channels and seizing assets hurts criminal organizations significantly.
- **Public-Private Partnership is Essential:** These operations rely on the cooperation of private sector entities like ISPs, hosting providers, and financial institutions to be successful.

---

## Mitigation Recommendations
While this article focuses on law enforcement action, the underlying criminal activities can be mitigated by standard cybersecurity best practices for organizations and individuals:
1.  **Defense in Depth:** Implement multiple layers of security controls (firewalls, EDR, email security) to protect against various threats.
2.  **User Education:** Train users to recognize and report phishing and other social engineering attempts.
3.  **Strong Authentication:** Use strong, unique passwords and enable MFA on all accounts.
4.  **Patch Management:** Keep all software and systems updated to protect against known vulnerabilities that malware often exploits.
