# New NERC Rule for Low-Impact Systems Creates Audit & Compliance Risks

**Severity:** medium | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Industrial Control Systems,Regulatory | **Updated:** 2026-10-27 | **Reading time:** 3 min

The NERC CIP-003-9 standard, effective since April 2026, is creating significant compliance and audit challenges for the North American energy sector. The rule imposes new, enforceable controls for vendor remote access to low-impact Bulk Electric System (BES) cyber systems, a category that previously lacked a minimum security baseline. A key issue is a discrepancy in recordkeeping requirements: entities must retain evidence for three years, but NERC audits can span a six-year period, potentially leaving operators unable to prove compliance for older activities and facing steep penalties.

## Executive Summary

A new cybersecurity standard from the **[North American Electric Reliability Corp. (NERC)](https://www.nerc.com)**, **CIP-003-9**, is causing significant compliance friction for owners and operators of low-impact Bulk Electric System (BES) cyber systems. The rule, which went into effect on April 1, 2026, mandates for the first time a minimum set of security controls for managing vendor electronic remote access to these systems. An analysis by the firm **[Centrii](https://www.centrii.com/)** highlights major challenges, particularly a mismatch between data retention requirements and audit lookback periods, which could expose energy companies to significant financial penalties and resource-intensive audit preparations.

---

## Regulatory Details

**NERC CIP-003-9** was developed after NERC's evaluation of over 1,000 entities revealed widespread and uncontrolled third-party remote access at low-impact sites, which include many renewable energy and storage facilities. The standard introduces three core enforceable requirements for these sites:

1.  **Identification**: A documented process to identify all instances of vendor electronic remote access.
2.  **Control**: A method to disable vendor remote access when it is not needed.
3.  **Monitoring**: A method to detect known or suspected malicious communications associated with that access.

Previously, low-impact BES cyber systems had no such mandatory baseline for remote access security, making this a major shift in compliance obligations.

---

## Affected Organizations

The rule directly affects all NERC-registered entities that own or operate low-impact BES cyber systems. This is a broad category that includes, but is not limited to:
-   Renewable energy facilities (solar, wind)
-   Energy storage sites
-   Smaller generation and transmission facilities

Responsibility for compliance remains with the registered entity, even if day-to-day operations and maintenance are outsourced to third-party contractors.

---

## Compliance Requirements

The most significant challenge identified by Centrii stems from a recordkeeping paradox. While the standard requires entities to retain evidence of compliance for **three calendar years**, NERC's compliance monitoring and enforcement program allows auditors to look back over a **six-year period** for low-impact requirements. This discrepancy creates a serious audit risk: an entity could be fully compliant with the three-year retention rule but unable to produce evidence for an audit query related to activity from four, five, or six years ago. This could result in a finding of non-compliance.

---

## Impact Assessment

The operational impact is substantial. Many entities have historically managed vendor access through informal means like emails, spreadsheets, or contractor-specific tools. Under CIP-003-9, they must now centralize, formalize, and document this entire process. The cost of non-compliance is steep. The **[Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC)](https://www.ferc.gov/)**, which provides NERC with its enforcement authority, can impose penalties of up to $1,584,648 per violation, per day. While negotiated settlements are often lower, the analysis suggests the largest cost for many will be the significant staff hours required to retroactively locate and organize compliance evidence for an audit.

---

## Compliance Guidance

To mitigate the risks associated with NERC CIP-003-9, affected entities should take immediate action:

1.  **Centralize Access Management**: Implement a centralized system for managing and logging all vendor remote access. This should be the single source of truth for all such connections.
2.  **Extend Retention Policies**: Adjust internal data retention policies to keep all relevant compliance evidence for a minimum of six years, aligning with the potential audit lookback period, not the three-year minimum.
3.  **Automate Evidence Collection**: Where possible, automate the logging of access requests, approvals, session activity, and disablement. This reduces the manual burden and creates a more reliable audit trail.
4.  **Vendor Contract Review**: Update contracts with third-party operators and vendors to ensure their processes and tools support your compliance obligations under CIP-003-9.
