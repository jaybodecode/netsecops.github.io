# CBIZ Proposes $7.2M Settlement for 2023 MOVEit Data Breach

**Severity:** medium | **Category:** Data Breach,Ransomware,Regulatory | **Updated:** 2026-10-31 | **Reading time:** 3 min

Business services firm CBIZ, Inc. has proposed a $7.2 million class-action settlement to compensate approximately 586,000 individuals affected by the May 2023 MOVEit data breach. The breach was carried out by the CL0P ransomware group, which exploited a zero-day vulnerability in the file transfer software. If approved, the settlement will provide reimbursement for losses, a cash payment option, and two years of free credit monitoring for class members. The agreement resolves claims against CBIZ, while litigation against MOVEit's developer, Progress Software, continues.

## Executive Summary
On October 30, 2026, a motion was filed for the preliminary approval of a $7.2 million class-action settlement between business services company **CBIZ, Inc.** and individuals impacted by a 2023 data breach. The breach stemmed from the exploitation of a zero-day vulnerability in Progress Software's **[MOVEit Transfer](https://www.progress.com/moveit)** application by the **[CL0P ransomware group](https://malpedia.caad.fkie.fraunhofer.de/actor/clop_ransomware_gang)**. The attack compromised the personal and health information of approximately 586,000 people. The proposed settlement, finalized on October 29, 2026, aims to compensate victims for financial losses and provide credit monitoring services, marking a significant step in the legal fallout from the widespread MOVEit hacking campaign.

## Regulatory Details
The settlement is part of the nationwide MOVEit multidistrict litigation (MDL) consolidated in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts. The breach occurred between May 27 and May 31, 2023, when CL0P systematically exfiltrated data from hundreds of organizations globally using the MOVEit flaw.

If the court grants preliminary approval, the settlement fund will be used to compensate class members under the following terms:
- **Ordinary Losses:** Reimbursement up to $2,500 for documented out-of-pocket expenses, including up to $100 for lost time.
- **Extraordinary Losses:** Reimbursement up to $10,000 for documented, significant financial harm directly resulting from the breach.
- **Alternative Cash Payment:** A one-time cash payment of $100, which may be adjusted based on the total number of claims filed.
- **Credit Monitoring:** All claimants will be eligible for two years of free credit monitoring and identity theft protection services.

This settlement resolves claims specifically against CBIZ and does not constitute an admission of wrongdoing. The broader litigation against Progress Software, the developer of MOVEit, is still ongoing.

## Affected Organizations
The direct victim organization is **CBIZ, Inc.**, but the breach impacted the 586,000 individuals whose sensitive data, including personal and health information, was stored on CBIZ's compromised MOVEit server.

## Impact Assessment
For CBIZ, the $7.2 million settlement represents a significant financial cost on top of the initial incident response and reputational damage. For the victims, the settlement provides a mechanism for financial recourse, although compensation for privacy loss is often difficult to quantify. The case is a prominent example of the long-tail legal and financial consequences that follow major data breaches, particularly those involving supply chain vulnerabilities like the one in MOVEit. It highlights the accountability organizations face for protecting the data entrusted to them, even when the breach originates from a third-party software flaw.

## Enforcement & Penalties
The $7.2 million settlement serves as the primary penalty for CBIZ in this class-action lawsuit. The final amount distributed to each claimant will depend on the number of claims filed and the types of losses claimed. The court's approval is required before the settlement is finalized and the claims process can begin.

## Compliance Guidance
This case serves as a critical reminder for organizations about third-party and supply chain risk management.
1.  **Vendor Risk Management:** Organizations must have a robust process for vetting the security of third-party software, especially for applications that handle sensitive data like MOVEit.
2.  **Rapid Patching:** When a critical vulnerability is announced, especially one with a known exploit, organizations must have a process to apply patches immediately. The MOVEit campaign demonstrated how quickly threat actors can weaponize a zero-day.
3.  **Data Minimization:** Organizations should regularly review and minimize the data stored on file transfer servers to reduce the potential impact of a breach.
