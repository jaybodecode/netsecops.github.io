# Ransomware Attacks Surge 50% in Q3 2026, Fueled by Gang Rivalry

**Severity:** high | **Category:** Ransomware,Threat Intelligence,Threat Actor | **Updated:** 2026-10-27 | **Reading time:** 3 min

Ransomware attacks increased by 50% year-over-year in the third quarter of 2026, with nearly 2,900 incidents documented in a new report from Nord Security. The surge is largely driven by intense competition between the two most prolific gangs, "The Gentlemen" and "Qilin," which were collectively responsible for 746 attacks. The report suggests the market has stabilized after previous law enforcement takedowns, entering a "new era" of escalating activity as groups vie for dominance in the cybercriminal underground.

## Executive Summary
A report from **[Nord Security](https://nordsecurity.com/)** published on October 21, 2026, reveals a significant escalation in ransomware activity during the third quarter of 2026. The research documented 2,894 attacks between July and September, marking a 50% increase compared to the same period in 2025 and an 8.6% rise from Q2 2026. This surge is primarily attributed to a fierce rivalry between the two most dominant ransomware groups, **The Gentlemen** and **Qilin**, who are competing for market share and notoriety. The findings indicate that the ransomware landscape is rebounding and intensifying after previous law enforcement disruptions, signaling a persistent and growing threat to organizations worldwide.

## Threat Landscape Analysis
The report highlights a stabilizing and increasingly competitive ransomware market. After major law enforcement actions against top-tier groups like LockBit and BlackCat in 2025, a power vacuum emerged. Now, new and existing groups are aggressively competing to fill that void. The 50% year-over-year increase is a clear indicator that ransomware remains a highly profitable and evolving criminal enterprise.

The primary engine of this growth is the intense competition between the top gangs. This rivalry incentivizes groups to innovate their TTPs, expand their affiliate networks, and increase their operational tempo to claim more victims and establish dominance.

## Key Threat Actors
The report identifies a clear hierarchy in the current ransomware ecosystem:
-   **The Gentlemen:** The most prolific group in Q3 2026, responsible for 383 documented attacks.
-   **[Qilin](https://malpedia.caad.fkie.fraunhofer.de/actor/qilin)**: A close second, linked to 363 attacks. Together, these two groups accounted for over 25% of all attributable incidents.
-   **Inc Ransom:** The third most active group, with 110 attacks.
-   **Other Notable Groups:** Several other gangs, including **Krybit**, **[Akira](https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G1024/)**, **Deadlock**, and **Clop Leaks**, each contributed nearly 100 attacks during the quarter, demonstrating a broad and active field of threat actors.

## Analyst Assessment
The data suggests that ransomware is not a waning threat but is instead entering a new phase of heightened activity. The rivalry between groups like The Gentlemen and Qilin acts as a catalyst for the entire ecosystem, driving innovation in both malware capabilities and extortion tactics (e.g., double and triple extortion). Organizations can expect to see more aggressive targeting, more sophisticated TTPs, and potentially shorter dwell times as these groups compete for speed and impact. The stabilization of the market implies that these high levels of activity are likely to be the new baseline for the foreseeable future.

## Impact Assessment
The direct impact of this surge is a greater number of organizations falling victim to disruptive and costly ransomware attacks. This leads to:
-   Increased financial losses from ransom payments, business interruption, and recovery costs.
-   A higher risk of data breaches as double extortion (data theft plus encryption) remains the standard tactic.
-   Greater strain on security teams who must defend against a larger volume and variety of attacks.

The report did not specify which industries were most affected, but historically, ransomware groups are opportunistic, targeting sectors like manufacturing, professional services, healthcare, and education.

## Mitigation Guidance
While the report focuses on attack trends, the fundamental defenses against ransomware remain consistent and more critical than ever:
1.  **Security Hygiene:** Focus on the basics, including strong password policies, regular software patching, and securing remote access services like RDP.
2.  **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):** Enforce MFA across all critical systems and accounts. This is a primary defense against initial access via compromised credentials.
3.  **Network Segmentation:** Divide the network into zones to prevent the rapid lateral spread of a ransomware infection.
4.  **Immutable Backups:** Maintain offline and immutable backups that cannot be deleted or encrypted by attackers. Regularly test your ability to restore from these backups.
5.  **User Education:** Train employees to recognize and report phishing, the most common initial infection vector for ransomware.
6.  **Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR):** Deploy EDR solutions to detect and respond to ransomware behaviors, such as mass file encryption and the deletion of shadow copies.
