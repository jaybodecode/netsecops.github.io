# ODVA's CIP Security Spec for EtherNet/IP Earns IEC 62443 Certification

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Industrial Control Systems,Policy and Compliance,Regulatory | **Updated:** 2026-10-27 | **Reading time:** 3 min

In a major step for industrial cybersecurity, the standards body ODVA announced its CIP Security specification for EtherNet/IP networks has been certified by TÜV Rheinland against the IEC 62443-4-2 standard. The specification achieved Security Level Capability 3 (SL-C 3), designed to protect against skilled industrial control system attackers. This certification provides a verified security baseline that is expected to significantly streamline the process for industrial device manufacturers to develop and certify their own secure-by-design products, helping them meet emerging regulations like the EU's Cyber Resilience Act.

## Executive Summary

**[ODVA](https://www.odva.org/)**, the industrial automation standards organization, has announced a significant milestone for operational technology (OT) security. Its **CIP Security** specification for EtherNet/IP networks has been formally certified by **[TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/)** as compliant with the **IEC 62443-4-2** international standard. The specification achieved a Security Level Capability 3 (SL-C 3), providing a robust and verified foundation for device authentication, data integrity, and confidentiality in industrial environments. This certification is poised to accelerate the adoption of secure industrial devices by simplifying the development and certification process for manufacturers and providing asset owners with a clear procurement standard.

---

## Regulatory Details

This certification aligns two critical industrial standards:

-   **CIP Security**: An extension to EtherNet/IP, one of the world's leading industrial networking protocols. It operates at the application layer to provide security services like device authentication, data integrity checks, access control, and optional encryption using **[TLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Layer_Security)** and DTLS.
-   **IEC 62443-4-2**: A globally recognized standard that defines the technical security requirements for individual components within an Industrial Automation and Control System (IACS), such as PLCs, drives, and I/O blocks.

Achieving **Security Level Capability 3 (SL-C 3)** is particularly important. This level requires a component to be resilient against 'intentional violation using sophisticated means with moderate resources, IACS specific skills and moderate motivation.' In essence, it is designed to thwart skilled OT attackers.

---

## Affected Organizations

This development primarily impacts two groups:

1.  **Industrial Device Manufacturers**: Companies that build products (drives, valves, controllers, etc.) using the EtherNet/IP protocol. They can now build upon a pre-certified specification, which should significantly reduce the time, cost, and complexity of getting their own products certified against IEC 62443-4-2.
2.  **Industrial Asset Owners**: Companies in sectors like manufacturing, energy, and water treatment that use these devices. They can now specify 'IEC 62443-4-2 SL-C 3' in their procurement requirements, ensuring they purchase equipment with a verified level of built-in security.

---

## Compliance Requirements

While the certification applies to the ODVA specification itself and does not automatically confer certification on products that implement it, it creates a clear pathway for compliance. Manufacturers must still undergo their own product certification process, but they can now do so with the confidence that the underlying security protocol meets the required standard. This is also a timely development for manufacturers selling into the **[European Union](https://european-union.europa.eu/)**, as it provides a powerful tool to help demonstrate compliance with the 'secure-by-design' principles mandated by the new **Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)**.

---

## Implementation Timeline

The announcement was made at ODVA's 2026 Industry Conference, which concluded on October 22. The certified specification is now available for vendors to begin integrating into their product development cycles. It is expected that a new wave of industrial devices built on this certified specification will begin to enter the market over the next 12-24 months.

---

## Impact Assessment

This certification is a significant positive step for the entire industrial ecosystem. By standardizing security at the protocol level, it helps to:

-   **Raise the Security Baseline**: It moves the industry away from insecure-by-default legacy protocols and towards a baseline of robust, built-in security.
-   **Simplify Procurement**: Asset owners no longer need to be security experts to choose secure components; they can simply require compliance with the standard.
-   **Reduce Development Costs**: It lowers the barrier to entry for manufacturers to produce secure products, fostering wider adoption of secure-by-design principles.
-   **Improve Regulatory Compliance**: It provides a clear mechanism for demonstrating adherence to emerging cybersecurity regulations like the CRA.
