# Airbus Moves Critical Apps to European Cloud to Avoid US CLOUD Act

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Cloud Security,Policy and Compliance,Regulatory | **Updated:** 2026-07-24 | **Reading time:** 4 min

Aerospace giant Airbus has selected French provider Scaleway for a sovereign cloud partnership, aiming to shield critical applications from non-European laws like the US CLOUD Act. In a significant strategic shift, Airbus made 'protection against extraterritorial legislation' a formal, scored criterion in its cloud vendor selection process. The move highlights a growing trend among European companies to prioritize digital sovereignty and legal jurisdiction as key components of their cloud security and risk management strategy.

## Executive Summary

European aerospace and defense leader **[Airbus](https://www.airbus.com/)** has made a strategic decision to partner with French cloud provider **Scaleway** to create a sovereign cloud environment for its critical applications. This move is explicitly designed to shield sensitive data from non-European extraterritorial laws, most notably the U.S. CLOUD Act. In a landmark shift in procurement strategy, Airbus formally included legal jurisdiction and protection from such laws as a scored criterion in its competitive tender, placing it on equal footing with technical capability and security. The initiative will start with the migration of 70 critical applications from providers like **[Amazon Web Services (AWS)](https://aws.amazon.com/)** and is part of a larger program to ensure the digital sovereignty of its industrial operations.

---

## Regulatory Details

The core of this story revolves around the **[U.S. CLOUD (Clarifying Lawful Overseas Use of Data) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLOUD_Act)**. This U.S. law, passed in 2018, asserts that U.S. law enforcement can compel U.S.-based technology companies to provide requested data, regardless of whether the data is stored in the U.S. or on foreign soil. This creates a direct conflict with data privacy regulations in other jurisdictions, such as the European Union's **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**.

For a multinational corporation like Airbus, which handles sensitive defense, aerospace, and personal data, the CLOUD Act presents a significant risk. It means that data stored with a U.S.-headquartered cloud provider (like AWS, Google Cloud, or Microsoft Azure) could be subject to access by U.S. authorities, even if that data resides in a European data center. Airbus's move is a direct risk mitigation strategy against this legal exposure.

## Affected Organizations

- **Primary:** Airbus, which is moving its applications.
- **Winner:** Scaleway, the cloud division of French telecom group Iliad, which won the contract.
- **Loser:** U.S.-based hyperscale cloud providers like AWS, which are losing business due to jurisdictional concerns.

This decision signals a trend that will affect all U.S. cloud providers operating in Europe and other regions with strong data privacy laws.

## Compliance Requirements

Airbus's action sets a new precedent for compliance and risk management in cloud procurement. For organizations handling sensitive data, especially in Europe, the following considerations are now critical:

1.  **Jurisdictional Risk Assessment:** Evaluating the legal jurisdiction of a cloud provider's parent company is as important as evaluating the physical location of its data centers.
2.  **Sovereign Cloud Solutions:** Prioritizing cloud providers that are owned, headquartered, and operated entirely within the desired legal jurisdiction (e.g., the EU) to ensure data is not subject to foreign laws.
3.  **Contractual Safeguards:** While helpful, contractual clauses with U.S. providers may not be sufficient to override the legal obligations imposed by the CLOUD Act. Therefore, structural solutions (i.e., choosing a non-U.S. provider) are becoming more common.

## Implementation Timeline

The initial phase involves the migration of approximately 70 critical applications. This is part of a broader program to bring around 900 applications, including ERP, CRM, and manufacturing systems, under a European sovereign cloud framework. A specific timeline for the full migration was not provided.

## Impact Assessment

- **For Airbus:** The move enhances its security and compliance posture by aligning its digital infrastructure with its legal and operational risk appetite. It gains greater control and resilience by reducing exposure to foreign legal frameworks.
- **For the Cloud Market:** This is a major validation for European sovereign cloud providers like Scaleway and a significant challenge to the dominance of U.S. hyperscalers. It forces the market to compete not just on technology and price, but also on legal and jurisdictional grounds.
- **For Digital Sovereignty:** Airbus's decision is a powerful statement in the global movement towards digital sovereignty, where nations and corporations seek to control their own digital destiny and protect their data from foreign government access.

## Compliance Guidance

Organizations, particularly in Europe, should review their cloud strategy in light of this development:
1.  **Data Classification:** Classify your data to identify which datasets are most sensitive to extraterritorial legal requests.
2.  **Vendor Risk Assessment:** Update your cloud vendor assessment criteria to include a scored section on legal jurisdiction and protection from foreign laws.
3.  **Hybrid/Multi-Cloud Strategy:** Consider a hybrid or multi-cloud strategy where the most sensitive data and applications are hosted on a sovereign cloud, while less sensitive workloads can remain with hyperscale providers to leverage their broader service offerings.
