# White House Launches 'Project Lifeline 250' to Secure Rural Hospitals

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Security Operations,Industrial Control Systems | **Updated:** 2026-10-28 | **Reading time:** 3 min

The White House, through the Office of the National Cyber Director (ONCD), has launched 'Project Lifeline 250,' a six-month pilot program designed to bolster the cybersecurity of underserved rural hospitals. Kicking off in South Carolina, the initiative is a public-private partnership providing free cybersecurity hardware, services, and support from technology companies like Microsoft, Cisco, and Zscaler to facilities that often lack the resources to defend against modern cyber threats.

## Executive Summary
On October 26, 2026, the **[White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)** Office of the National Cyber Director (ONCD) and the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) announced the launch of **Project Lifeline 250**. This new pilot program aims to provide free, robust cybersecurity resources to rural hospitals, which are critical infrastructure but often lack the budget and personnel to defend against cyberattacks. The six-month initiative, first launching in South Carolina, partners the federal government with leading technology firms including **[Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/security)**, **[Cisco](https://www.cisco.com)**, **[Zscaler](https://www.zscaler.com/)**, **Trellix**, **Cyera**, and **Abnormal AI**. The program will deliver essential hardware, software, and security services at no cost to participating hospitals, extending a model previously tested for water utilities.

---

## Regulatory Details
Project Lifeline 250 is not a regulatory mandate but an assistance program designed to uplift the security posture of the nation's most vulnerable healthcare providers. The program is built on a public-private partnership model and has three primary objectives:

1.  **Strengthen Network Defenses:** Deploy modern security tools and services to protect hospital networks from common threats like ransomware and phishing.
2.  **Ensure Operational Resilience:** Provide physical backup hardware to ensure that critical patient care can continue even during a significant cyber incident or outage.
3.  **Remediate Legacy Risks:** Identify and develop plans to replace insecure legacy technologies and high-risk medical devices, which are often a primary entry point for attackers.

The program was kicked off by National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross and South Carolina Governor Henry McMaster at Newberry Health Hospital. It is designed to be scalable, with the goal of using the pilot's findings to develop a broader national program.

---

## Affected Organizations
The initial phase of Project Lifeline 250 will focus on an unspecified number of rural hospitals within **South Carolina**. These facilities are often characterized as having limited IT budgets and a shortage of cybersecurity expertise, making them attractive targets for threat actors. The average hospital has been cited as having over 26,000 internet-exposed devices, creating a massive and difficult-to-manage attack surface that this program aims to address.

---

## Compliance Requirements
There are no direct compliance requirements for hospitals to join; participation is voluntary. The program is structured as a grant of services and technology rather than a set of rules to be followed. The long-term goal is to establish a baseline of security that could inform future best practices or standards for the healthcare sector, but the current focus is on immediate risk reduction.

---

## Implementation Timeline
Project Lifeline 250 is a **six-month pilot program**. The initial phase in South Carolina will be used to evaluate the effectiveness of the provided solutions and the public-private partnership model. Based on this evaluation, the ONCD will determine how to scale the program to other states and protect more communities nationwide.

### Partner Contributions:
- **Trellix:** Will provide threat detection and response services, including those powered by agentic AI.
- **Cyera:** Will assist hospitals with data inventory and classification to identify where sensitive patient data resides.
- **Microsoft:** Will leverage its experience from supporting over 870 rural hospitals to provide security solutions.
- **Cisco, Zscaler, Abnormal AI:** Will provide a range of hardware, network security, and email security services.

---

## Impact Assessment
This initiative directly addresses a well-documented national security vulnerability. Rural hospitals are frequent targets of ransomware attacks due to their critical nature and perceived inability to invest in robust defenses. A successful attack can lead to canceled surgeries, diverted patients, and, in the worst cases, negative patient outcomes. By providing enterprise-grade security tools and expertise for free, Project Lifeline 250 can significantly raise the defensive bar for these facilities. The program's success could create a replicable model for protecting other under-resourced critical infrastructure sectors across the United States.
