# DOE and INL Launch Cyber-Informed Engineering Initiative for Energy Sector

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Industrial Control Systems,Security Operations | **Updated:** 2026-10-14 | **Reading time:** 3 min

The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) and Idaho National Laboratory (INL) have launched a new initiative to promote Cyber-Informed Engineering (CIE). The program aims to embed cybersecurity principles into the design phase of energy infrastructure, enhancing resilience against cyberattacks by leveraging engineered, physics-based controls rather than relying solely on traditional IT security measures.

## Executive Summary
The U.S. Department of Energy's (**[DOE](https://www.energy.gov/)**) Office of Cybersecurity, Energy Security, and Emergency Response (CESER) and **[Idaho National Laboratory (INL)](https://inl.gov/)** have launched a new educational initiative called "Cyber-Informed Engineering (CIE) Back-to-School Weekly." This program is designed to fundamentally shift the energy sector's approach to cybersecurity by integrating security into the engineering and design lifecycle of critical infrastructure. The goal is to build systems that are inherently resilient to cyber threats, capable of preventing catastrophic failures even if digital defenses are breached.

---

## Program Details
The initiative provides a structured learning path for energy professionals and engineers to adopt the 12 foundational principles of CIE. Delivered through a dedicated learning hub, the program will release new content weekly, with each module focusing on a specific principle, complete with a case study and practical application questions. The first module covers "Consequence-Focused Design," which prioritizes protecting against the most severe potential outcomes of an attack.

To further support adoption, CESER and INL have developed supplementary resources:
- A CIE Implementation Guide
- A web-based CIE Analysis Tool
- A forthcoming CIE Handbook to serve as a simplified entry point

This educational push is complemented by a $100 million funding opportunity via the Rural and Municipal Utility Cybersecurity (RMUC) program, aimed at bolstering the security posture of smaller electric utilities.

## Affected Organizations
This initiative is primarily targeted at professionals and organizations within the U.S. energy sector, including:
- Engineers and system designers
- Critical infrastructure owners and operators
- Rural and municipal electric utilities
- Federal and state energy regulators

## Core Principles of CIE
Cyber-Informed Engineering is built on the premise that cyberattacks on critical infrastructure are inevitable and that digital defenses alone are insufficient. CIE seeks to mitigate these risks by designing systems with engineered controls that can prevent destructive consequences. This involves thinking like an adversary to anticipate attack paths and building in physical or engineering-based safeguards. For example, designing a chemical process with a non-digital pressure release valve that prevents an explosion even if the control system is compromised.

## Implementation Timeline
The "CIE Back-to-School Weekly" series began on October 14, 2026, and will continue with weekly content releases. A new CIE Handbook will be published at the conclusion of the series. The RMUC funding opportunity is also currently active, providing financial support for smaller utilities to adopt these and other cybersecurity measures.

## Impact Assessment
The CIE initiative represents a significant strategic shift for the security of U.S. critical infrastructure. By moving security considerations "left" into the design phase, it aims to create a new generation of energy systems that are more resilient by nature. This reduces the reliance on bolt-on, reactive security measures and can lower the risk of catastrophic physical events resulting from cyberattacks. For smaller utilities, the associated funding provides a crucial opportunity to access technical assistance and technologies that might otherwise be out of reach.

## Guidance for Adoption
Organizations in the energy sector are encouraged to:
1.  **Engage with the Program**: Participate in the weekly learning series and utilize the CIE Analysis Tool and other resources provided by DOE and INL.
2.  **Cross-functional Collaboration**: Foster collaboration between engineering teams, IT security, and operational technology (OT) security teams to integrate CIE principles into new projects.
3.  **Apply for Funding**: Eligible rural and municipal utilities should explore the $100 million RMUC funding opportunity to finance cybersecurity improvements.
4.  **Adopt a Consequence-Driven Mindset**: Prioritize security efforts based on mitigating the worst possible physical outcomes of a cyberattack, not just on preventing initial access.
