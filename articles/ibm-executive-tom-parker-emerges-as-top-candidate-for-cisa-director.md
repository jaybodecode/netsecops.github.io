# IBM Executive Tom Parker Reportedly a Top Candidate for CISA Director

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Regulatory,Other | **Updated:** 2026-05-04 | **Reading time:** 3 min

Tom Parker, a cybersecurity executive at IBM, is reportedly a leading candidate to be the next director of the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA). According to sources familiar with the matter, Parker's extensive private-sector background is a key attribute favored by the current administration and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. Parker, who has no prior government experience, founded the cyber solutions firm Hubble and is a regular speaker at the Black Hat conference. The CISA leadership position has been without a permanent director since the previous nominee withdrew, and while Parker is seen as a frontrunner, the selection process remains fluid.

## Executive Summary

**Tom Parker**, a security services lead at **[IBM](https://www.ibm.com/)**, has reportedly emerged as a frontrunner for the director position at the **[U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)](https://www.cisa.gov)**. Sources indicate that the current administration, and specifically Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, favor a candidate with a strong, exclusive background in the private sector. Parker, who has nearly two decades of private-sector experience, founded the cyber firm Hubble and is a recognized figure in the industry. His potential nomination would bring a non-government perspective to the leadership of the nation's top civilian cybersecurity agency. The selection process is ongoing and no official announcement has been made.

---

## Policy Details

This news concerns a potential leadership change at a key U.S. government agency. The Director of CISA is a critical role responsible for leading the national effort to protect critical infrastructure from physical and cyber threats.

- **Agency**: Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), part of the Department of Homeland Security.
- **Position**: Director of CISA.
- **Candidate**: Tom Parker, an executive at IBM Security.
- **Key Consideration**: The administration's reported preference for a leader with an entirely private-sector background, a departure from past appointments that often included individuals with extensive government or military experience.

---

## Affected Organizations

- **Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)**: The agency's leadership, direction, and priorities would be shaped by the new director.
- **U.S. Government**: The overall national cybersecurity strategy would be influenced by CISA's leadership.
- **Private Sector**: The nature of public-private partnerships, a cornerstone of CISA's mission, could evolve under new leadership.
- **IBM**: The potential departure of a key executive.

---

## Impact Assessment

The appointment of a CISA director with an exclusively private-sector background could have several impacts:

- **Pros**: A private-sector leader might bring a more agile, innovative, and business-oriented approach to the agency. They may be better equipped to strengthen partnerships with the tech industry and understand the challenges faced by private companies.
- **Cons**: A lack of government experience could present a steep learning curve in navigating the complexities of federal bureaucracy, inter-agency politics, and the specific legal and regulatory authorities that govern CISA's operations.

Parker's background as a founder of a cyber firm (Hubble) and his recognition as a technology pioneer at the World Economic Forum suggest a focus on innovation and technical solutions. His frequent appearances at industry conferences like Black Hat indicate strong ties to the cybersecurity research community.

---

## Implementation Timeline

The position of CISA Director requires Senate confirmation. If nominated, Tom Parker would undergo a vetting process and confirmation hearings. The timeline for this process can vary significantly depending on the political climate and the Senate's schedule.
