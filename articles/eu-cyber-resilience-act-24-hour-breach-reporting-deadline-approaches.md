# EU Cyber Resilience Act's 24-Hour Breach Reporting Deadline Looms

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Regulatory | **Updated:** 2026-09-11 | **Reading time:** 4 min

A key compliance milestone for the EU's Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) is approaching on September 11, 2026. From that date, manufacturers of products with digital elements sold in the EU market must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents to authorities within 24 hours of becoming aware of them. This stringent deadline marks a significant shift towards mandatory, rapid disclosure and requires organizations to have mature incident response processes in place.

## Executive Summary
Manufacturers, importers, and distributors of products with digital elements in the **[European Union](https://european-union.europa.eu/index_en)** are facing a critical deadline. Starting September 11, 2026, the EU's Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) will enforce a strict 24-hour reporting requirement for certain cybersecurity events. This regulation mandates that manufacturers notify relevant authorities of any actively exploited vulnerability or severe security incident within 24 hours of awareness. This impending obligation represents a major regulatory shift, moving from voluntary disclosure to a legally binding, rapid-response framework that will require significant process maturity from affected organizations.

## Regulatory Details
The Cyber Resilience Act, which officially entered into force in December 2024, aims to bolster the cybersecurity of digital products across the EU. While its full set of requirements will not be applicable until December 2027, the reporting obligations are being fast-tracked.

The multi-stage reporting timeline is as follows:
1.  **Early Warning (24 Hours)**: Within 24 hours of becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or a severe incident, the manufacturer must submit an initial "early warning" notification to the relevant authority (such as ENISA or a national CSIRT).
2.  **Detailed Notification (72 Hours)**: A more detailed report on the incident or vulnerability must be provided within 72 hours.
3.  **Final Report (14 Days / 1 Month)**: A final report is due within 14 days of a patch or mitigation becoming available for a vulnerability, or within one month for a severe incident.

> This 24-hour rule applies to products already on the market, not just new ones, creating a significant retroactive compliance burden.

## Affected Organizations
The CRA has a broad scope, affecting a wide range of economic operators involved in the lifecycle of "products with digital elements." This includes:
*   **Manufacturers**: The primary party responsible for compliance, from software developers to hardware makers.
*   **Importers**: Entities that place a product from a third country on the EU market.
*   **Distributors**: Entities in the supply chain, other than the manufacturer or importer, who make a product available on the market.

Essentially, any company producing or selling items like smart devices, software, or IoT products within the EU will be impacted.

## Compliance Requirements
To meet the September 11, 2026 deadline, organizations must have the following capabilities in place:
*   A robust process for monitoring and identifying security incidents and vulnerabilities in their products.
*   A clear internal framework for assessing the severity of an incident and determining if it meets the threshold for an "actively exploited vulnerability" or "severe incident."
*   A well-defined and tested incident response plan that includes a specific workflow for gathering the necessary information and submitting the 24-hour early warning report to the correct authorities.
*   Legal and technical teams prepared to collaborate under extreme time pressure.

## Implementation Timeline
*   **December 2024**: Cyber Resilience Act enters into force.
*   **September 11, 2026**: The 24-hour reporting obligation for vulnerabilities and incidents begins.
*   **December 2027**: The full Cyber Resilience Act becomes applicable.

## Impact Assessment
The 24-hour reporting window will place immense pressure on organizations. The key impacts include:
*   **Operational Strain**: Security and legal teams will need to be on standby to respond to incidents at any time to meet the deadline.
*   **Increased Transparency**: The act forces rapid public (or semi-public) acknowledgment of security issues, reducing the time companies have to investigate privately.
*   **Resource Allocation**: Organizations will need to invest in tools, personnel, and processes for vulnerability management, incident response, and regulatory reporting.
*   **Legal and Financial Risk**: Failure to comply can result in significant financial penalties and legal action.

## Enforcement & Penalties
While not detailed in the source articles, EU regulations like the GDPR have set a precedent for substantial fines for non-compliance, often calculated as a percentage of global annual revenue. Similar enforcement mechanisms are expected for the CRA.

## Compliance Guidance
Organizations should take the following steps to prepare:
1.  **Conduct a Gap Analysis**: Assess your current incident response and vulnerability disclosure policies against the CRA's requirements.
2.  **Develop a CRA Playbook**: Create a specific, step-by-step playbook for handling a CRA-reportable incident. This should define roles, responsibilities, communication channels, and the exact information required for the 24-hour report.
3.  **Tabletop Exercises**: Regularly conduct tabletop exercises that simulate a zero-day vulnerability discovery to test your playbook and ensure your team can meet the 24-hour deadline.
4.  **Identify Reporting Channels**: Determine which national CSIRT or authority you will need to report to in each relevant EU member state.
