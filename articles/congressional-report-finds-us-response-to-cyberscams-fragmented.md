# Congressional Report Finds US Response to Cyberscams 'Fragmented'

**Severity:** informational | **Category:** Policy and Compliance,Regulatory,Phishing | **Updated:** 2026-10-31 | **Reading time:** 4 min

A congressional investigation by the Joint Economic Committee has concluded that the U.S. government's efforts to combat a global surge in cyberscams are 'fragmented' and ineffective. The report highlights that federal agencies lack a unified strategy, fail to hold the private sector accountable, and recover only a tiny fraction of the nearly $18 billion lost by American victims in 2025. The committee recommends new legislation to create a centralized reporting system and a coordinated federal strategy.

## Executive Summary
A new report from the Democrats on the **[U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/)**'s Joint Economic Committee (JEC) has delivered a scathing assessment of the federal government's response to the escalating crisis of global cyberscams. The investigation, released on October 27, 2026, found the U.S. effort to be "dangerously fragmented" and largely ineffective, resulting in devastating financial losses for American citizens. The report highlights a lack of a unified strategy among federal agencies, a failure to hold private sector companies accountable for their role in facilitating scams, and an abysmal rate of fund recovery for victims. With Americans losing nearly $18 billion to online fraud in 2025, the committee is urging legislative action to overhaul the nation's approach to combating this pervasive threat.

## Regulatory Details
The JEC investigation, led by Senator Maggie Hassan and Representative Don Beyer, identified several critical failures in the current U.S. government framework:
- **Lack of Coordination:** There is no single, coordinated strategy across federal agencies like the **[FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/)**, Secret Service, and State Department. Agencies do not even maintain a unified accounting of funds lost, seized, or returned.
- **Poor Recovery Rates:** The amount of money returned to victims is a minuscule fraction of the losses. The FBI reported returning just $336 million of the nearly $18 billion lost in 2025.
- **No Private Sector Accountability:** Unlike the UK and EU, the U.S. has not established liability for financial institutions or social media platforms that fail to prevent the abuse of their services for fraud. This lack of incentive hinders proactive prevention efforts.
- **Insufficient International Engagement:** The report notes that State Department funding cuts eliminated an anti-scam training program in Bangladesh, a country identified as an emerging hub for scam operations.

To address these deficiencies, the report calls on Congress to pass the bipartisan ReportScams.Gov Act, which would mandate a unified federal strategy and create a centralized portal for victim reporting and assistance.

## Affected Organizations
The report primarily scrutinizes the performance of U.S. government agencies, including the Joint Economic Committee, FBI, Secret Service, and Department of State. However, the ultimate victims are the American public, and the report also implicates private sector entities like banks and social media companies for not doing enough to prevent fraud.

## Impact Assessment
The financial and emotional toll on American citizens is immense, with losses amounting to over $2 million every hour in 2025. The report concludes that the current fragmented government response provides little hope for victims seeking to recover their money. This failure not only harms individuals but also erodes trust in digital platforms and financial institutions. Furthermore, the unchecked flow of funds to transnational criminal organizations, often operating from hostile or uncooperative nations, fuels a global criminal ecosystem and may have national security implications.

## Enforcement & Penalties
A key finding of the report is the distinct lack of enforcement and penalties, particularly for private sector platforms that are exploited by scammers. The report's authors argue that without financial liability or stronger regulatory pressure, these companies have little incentive to invest significantly in anti-fraud measures. The proposed legislation aims to create a more robust framework where both government and industry are held more accountable.

## Compliance Guidance
While the report is directed at government policy, it contains important takeaways for businesses:
- **Proactive Fraud Prevention:** Financial institutions and tech platforms should anticipate increased regulatory pressure to implement stronger anti-fraud controls. This includes better user verification, transaction monitoring for scam-like patterns, and faster response to takedown requests.
- **Victim Support:** Companies whose platforms are used for scams should develop clearer and more supportive processes for victims to report incidents and receive assistance.
- **Information Sharing:** The private sector should enhance information sharing with law enforcement and each other to more quickly identify and disrupt large-scale scam campaigns.
