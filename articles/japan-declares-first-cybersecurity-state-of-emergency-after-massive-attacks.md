# Japan Declares First-Ever Cybersecurity State of Emergency

**Severity:** critical | **Category:** Cyberattack,Ransomware,Policy and Compliance | **Updated:** 2026-10-16 | **Reading time:** 1 min

The Japanese government has declared its first "cybersecurity state of emergency" following a catastrophic wave of cyberattacks that have affected nearly 500 businesses and exposed the personal data of around 14 million people. A major catalyst was a ransomware attack against cloud provider IDC Frontier, which caused a cascading failure impacting 495 of its clients. Officials suspect the attacks are being facilitated by advanced AI tools that help criminals overcome previous language barriers, allowing them to target Japan's legacy infrastructure more effectively. The declaration signals a national-level crisis and a mobilization of resources to combat the escalating cyber threats.

## Executive Summary

In an unprecedented response to a wave of crippling cyberattacks, the **Japanese government** has declared its first-ever **"cybersecurity state of emergency."** The decision, made by the National Cybersecurity Strategy Headquarters (NCSH), comes after a series of devastating ransomware and data breach incidents in September and October 2026. These attacks have impacted nearly 500 companies and exposed the personal information of an estimated **14 million citizens**.
